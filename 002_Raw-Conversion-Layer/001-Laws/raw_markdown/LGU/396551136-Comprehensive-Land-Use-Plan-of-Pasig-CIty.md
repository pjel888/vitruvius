##### A. RATIONALE

The recent growth development in the country and Metro Manila, including Pasig and the outlying areas had not only added a new role for the City of Pasig but also demanded a change in its traditional role to the metropolitan and national structure.

From an industrialized city, it now moves towards becoming the business and financial capital of the metropolis and the country as well. From municipality, which has attained full growth and development from its own objectives and goals, it has evolved into a city, which must function not only within but also beyond the bounds of metropolitan structure in the light of an expanding environment of growth and development throughout the country.

City hood, realized in 1994, through the effort of Mayor Vicente P. Eusebio brought along new changes and nurture new dreams and aspirations on the hearts and minds of the people. New hope, new opportunities, new generation faces the city.

##### B. MANDATE

Republic Act 7160, the Local Government Code of 1991 mandates local government units to adopt a comprehensive land use plan and enact zoning ordinance.

Republic Act 7279, Urban Development and Housing Act (UDHA) also mandates local government units under Section 6 and 39 to prepare a comprehensive land use plan in pursuance of the objectives of UDHA

##### C. BRIEF DECRIPTION

Pasig is one of the 13 cities and 4 towns compromising the Metropolitan Manila area. It used to be the capital of the then Premier Province until the creation of Metropolitan Manila.

It acquires its city hood status in December 1994 by virtue of Republic Act 7829 converting Pasig into a highly urbanized City.

Thirty (30) barangays comprise the city of Pasig with an aggregate area of 3,100 hectares and total population of 471,075 (1995 official census). Pasig City 617,301 (estimated as of 2018)

In the Metro Manila structure, Pasig is located at the eastern boundary of Metro-Manila and within the intermediate core, together with Muntinlupa, Valenzuela, Las Piñas, Paranaque, Marikina, Tagig, Pateros.

In the national structure, it lies within the Laguna de Bay basin and between two coastal growth centers of Metro Manila and MARILAQUE

It lies approximately on the southern end of Pasig River and is bounded by Quezon City and Marikina City on the North; the City of Mandaluyong on the West; Pateros, Tagig and Makati City on the South; and Rizal towns of Cainta and Taytay on the East.

##### A) LAND USE HISTORY

Land use changes in the City of Pasig from mere residential town of the Province of Rizal came about with the influx of industries during sixties and seventies, signaling the start of the urbanization process of Pasig. The single biggest factor affecting this land use change has been attributed to the aggressiveness of the local government in attracting investments in industry characterized mainly by tax incentives and laxity in development regulations and controls.

Loose Regulations led to large scale industries setting any where mostly along the Pasig and Marikina River, now registering about 200. Industrialization made Pasig one of the greatest recipients of migration, while absence of development controls and use regulations resulted in indiscriminate land use that brought about urban adversities as traffic congestion, population, squatting and flooding among others. While industrialization increased the number of population, housing components in industrial development had been neglected that resulted, aside from squatting, in the existence and

prevalence of housing settlements characterized by temporary land tenure arrangement, ie, unplanned communities. Population more than doubled in 1970 from 1960 level.

Industries had the time of flourish and abundance until the second half of the last decade when a new form of urban development took shape at the start of the eighties with the development of the Ortigas Center, a private sector initiative. The Development of the Ortigas Center not only set a new era in land use and development for the City of Pasig, but also set the pace for the transformation of Pasig into a highly urbanized city.

Halfway through this decade, the fourth since the start of the urbanization, City hood became a reality. In 1994, through the efforts of Mayor Vicente P. Eusebio, Republic Act 7829 converting the Municipality of Pasig into a highly urbanized city was passed by Congress and ratified by the people of Pasig in a plebiscite held in January 21, 1995.

City hood created opportunities for urban expansion. It attracted and opened the floodgates of foreign investments particularly in upscale condominium development. It added a new dimension in land development. The rising Skyline of the Ortigas Center is now dominating the city’s landscape. It is now influencing development not only on the fringes but also on the entire city. Commerce and trade has taken over the industries as frontrunner/front liner of the economy.

Projection for Pasig shows a population of 532,394 for year 2000, an increase of 61,619 over 1995 figure. Population is projected to reach 628,428 in year 2010.

Barangay San Antonio, among all barangays, has the lowest density with 36 persons per hectare. This is where the Ortigas Center is located.

##### C) FINANCE

The year 2000 income of the city is placed at P 2.1 B., the 4th in rank among Metro Manila towns and cities, an increase of 1260% over 1992 level, the year of the passage of the local government code.

From 1974, the city’s income rose from P 18.4 M to P 60.8 M in 1987, with an average increase of 50% every 5 years or 10% every year.

From 1987, income rose to P 154.3 M, a dramatic increase of 153% over five (5) year period or an average of 30% per year. These years saw the start of the Post-Edsa era.

In 1993, however with the start of implementation of the local government code, income rose to P 487 M, an unprecedented increase of 215% over income of 1992. The new tax code of the City of Pasig was implemented in 1993.

Since 1993, income grew at an annual increase of 27% reaching P 2.1 B in year 2000. Almost 50% of the income comes from taxes, particularly real property and business taxes, while about 19% comes from the internal revenue allotment. The rest comes from operating and service income revenues.

##### E) ENVIRONMENT

##### 1. Soil and Topography

The geographical division of Metro Manila has placed Pasig under the Marikina Valley Zone. As such, soil composition is generally characterized by Marikina Clay loam representing 86% of the total land area. About 14% is within the Guadalupe Plateau Zone where soil is very suitable for urban development. Valle Verde Subdivisions, Barangay Kapitolyo and the Ortigas Center stand on this zone. Pasig is generally flat, characterized by level to undulating slopes with gradients ranging from 0-5%. All barangays have 0-2 percent slope, except Bagong Ilog, Pineda, Kapitolyo, and Oranbo.

##### 2. Land and Water Resources

Total land area is 31 sq. km. or 3,100 hectares, roughly 5% of the Metropolitan Manila area. It ranks 10th in terms of land area among other towns and cities of

Metro Manila. Barangay Ugong occupies the largest area, about 12% of the total land area, while Barangay Sto. Tomas occupies the smallest area with 6%.

Two main natural bodies of water traverse the City and influencing development, the Pasig River and the Marikina River. Approximately 16 km. run through Pasig. In addition, 17 creeks/esteros run through Pasig territory and its periphery. Another body of water, manmade Manggahan Floodway cuts across the City, from the Marikina River bend in Baranggay Rosario towards Laguna Bay.

###### b) Residential

In terms of land uses, Pasig is still predominantly residential with residential areas occupying approximately 1,815 hectares of land. Residential subdivisions are mostly average to middle income class with concentrations on the western and northern part of Pasig mostly medium density residential development. High density residential areas are mostly located on the city proper on the southern part. The largest subdivision and also the only low density subdivision in Pasig, the Valle Verde Subdivision developed by Ortigas & Company occupies about 80 hectares of land in the

western entrance adjacent to the Ortigas Center.

In all case, residential development are mostly single types dwelling with very few instances of row house type of housing. Even less are medium rise types of housing. Rarely has the private sectors venture into medium rise housing development similar to what was done by the RFM which developed a portion of its industrial property in Barangay Manggahan into a medium rise residential community.

###### c) Commercial

Commercial uses, occupy 7% of the land are prominent of which is the Ortigas Center, the Central Business District (CBD) of Pasig. Other secondary commercial activities are located in the public market and its vicinity and in the commercial strips along Ortigas Avenue, Shaw Boulevard, and A. Mabini St. (Poblacion). Shopping blocks or pedestrian mall type of shopping centers

are provided by the Ever Gotesco Complex, Makro, Robinson Complex and Sta. Lucia Mall located in the northern portion of Pasig, and by Pioneer Shopping Center at the western portion of Pasig.

Commercial establishments in the form of warehouses lately have proliferated indicating the preference of Pasig for storage and stocking by Metro Manila establishments.

###### d) Industrial

Industrial uses account for 15% of the total land area mostly located in Barangay Ugong, Bagong-Ilog, Rosario and Manggahan. Approximately 200 large industries, both light and heavy, exist in the city, mostly engaged in the manufacturing of food, textile, garments, chemical products, and electronics.

###### e) Parks

No system of parks exists in Pasig, except the barangay park system. A 6hectare lot, locally known as the Rainforest, owned and maintained by the City of Pasig constitutes the Central Park of Pasig. This park is provided with plant nurseries, multi-purpose center and sports facilities, such as swimming pool, sports oval and a gym.

Pasig has a barangay center in each of the 30 barangays. Aside from barangay halls and/or multipurpose centers constructed thereon, most are equipped with basketball court and/or other play courts such as tennis courts at Barangay Rosario (Doña Damiana Subdivision) and at Barangay San Nicolas. However, with the exception of some plazas and subdivision park, green parks on these barangays are still much to be desired.

The Phil Sports (ULTRA) and the Rosario Sports Complex are 2 large recreational centers in Pasig. Both are venues for national competition. In

addition, the Rizal High School, al rage public high school located along the banks of Marikina-River in Barangay Caniogan, is provided with a large park and sports oval where local and regional school sports competitions are often held.

Approximately 10 hectares of land are used as burial grounds: the Roman Catholic cemetery, the City Government’s cemetery, Santolan cemetery and the Evergreen Memorial Park. These are concentrated on or near the City Center.

###### f) Agricultural/Open Spaces

Pasig is left with only about 15% of vacant and agricultural spaces available for development expansion. Some of these spaces include the 18 hectare PCGG property and the 15 hectare property of the Rizal Provincial Government, both Prime lands in Barangay Ugong and large tracts of land

at the Pasig-Cainta-Taytay boundary along the Pasig River. There are also open spaces surrounding the Mutya ng Pasig public market that are open for commercial development.

There is a very insignificant area devoted to agricultural purposes mostly planted with vegetables. Rice production is limited to personal consumption. Most of the open spaces and agricultural lands are located on the southeastern side of Pasig within the Laguna Bay basin.

###### g) Institutional

Large institutions in Pasig are the Rizal Medical Center; Pasig City General Hospital, Rizal Provincial Capitol, Rizal High School, St. Paul’s College, Pasig Catholic College, the Roman Catholic church, Meralco, and the 5 hectare Government Center in Barangay San Nicolas that houses the City Hall, Sports Center, Convention Center and offices of national government agencies, as the

Bureau of Internal Revenue, Eastern Police District and the Post Office.

###### 1.Road Structure

Total road network traversing the City of Pasig is approximately 260 km. including private subdivision roads, 12.23% are national and provincial roads. There are two (2) roads providing directional routes along territory of Pasig; A. Rodriguez-Dr. Sixto Antonio route along the North-South axis and Ortigas Avenue along East-West

Axis. These routes provide accessibility to outer and adjacent towns and cities. Roads and major thoroughfares are mostly concrete and in good condition.

There are 4 major thoroughfares of Metro Manila that pass thru City of Pasig and these are the circumferential road C5, Ortigas Avenue, Shaw Blvd, and Marcos Highway.

There are two (2) interchanges in the City of Pasig both located along C-5, at Vargas Intersection and at Ortigas intersection.

###### 2.Bridges

There are eleven (11) major bridges in the city, four (4) across Marikina River, three (3) across Pasig River and three (3) across Manggahan Floodway. Another bridge, Buting bridge crosses the Pateros River and connects

Pasig to Makati. There are several other minor bridges that serve as barangay linkages across creeks/esteros all around the City.

###### 3.Approaches

Main approach to Pasig from the west is through Ortigas Avenue, Shaw Boulevard, and Kalayaan Avenue. These three (3) major thoroughfares pass thru three (3) major bridges over Marikina River, Rosario Bridge, Vargas Bridge and Buting Bridge, along the route towards the East and the innermost part of the city, including the poblacion. Approach from the north is through Amang Rodriguez Avenue and from the South is through Dr. Sixto Antonio Avenue. These two primary roads connect to from North-South corridor of Pasig.

###### 4.Public Transport

Most common means of travel is through jeepneys, for travel outside the city and through tricycles for in-city travel. Public buses are not common, although there are some that pass thru the city via Ortigas

Avenue plying Manila-Rizal route. The FX vehicles, called the poor man’s taxi now become an acceptable and affordable substitute for taxis among MM and suburban commuters and residents.

###### 5.Pedestrian overpasses

There are three (3) pedestrian overpasses within the city. These are located at Shaw Blvd. in front of the Rizal Medical Center, at C-5 Valle Verde and at Ortigas Avenue near Rosario Bridge.

##### G)FLOOD CONTROL FACILITIES

Drainage lines within the City of Pasig has a total aggregate length of 104 kilometers consisting of open canal, covered canal and underground reinforced pipes. This represents the drainage facilities installed in private subdivision which are mostly underground reinforced concrete pipes.

Almost all thoroughfares and residential areas have underground drainage facilities, while most high density residential areas are provided with open or closed canal to relieved the city of storm flooding.

Tow major waterways, Pasig River and Marikina River and sixteen (16) other creeks serve as the city’s drainage outfall. A total of 26 km. of creeks runs through the territory and periphery of Pasig. Another major waterway, the man-made Manggahahn Floodway, together with its flood control mechanism was constructed in 1980 as major component of Metropolitan Flood Control Program.

At present, Pasig has three (3) pumping stations located on both ends of the Parian Creek in Ilugin, Pinagbuhatan and Kapasigan and at San Agustin creek, Barangay Pinagbuhatan with combined pumping capacity of 10 gallons/minute.

##### H. CURRENT PLANS & PROGRAMS

###### 1. Maripas Clusters Plan

This is an Infrastructure Plan formulated in 1998 jointly by the Province of Rizal, and the cities of Pasig and Marikina basically to improve traffic situation. The Plan recognizes the traffic interrelationship within the three (3) local government units and the need for coordinated effort to address these issues. The plan for Pasig calls for development of new travel routes to the Eastern Province of Rizal via Mercedes Avenue in Barangay San Miguel and/or via Elisco road in Barangay Kalawaan/San Joaquin

###### 2. Socio Economic Development

Plan

This was formulated by the city of Pasig in 1995 with technical assistance from NEDA. The plan consists of tri-modal Sectoral plans for health, education. Economic, infrastructure, social welfare, protective services and development administration.

###### 3. Pasig River Rehabilitation

###### Program

This program is being managed and implemented by the Pasig River Rehabilitation Commission of which the City of Pasig is a member, for the rehabilitation of the Pasig River and its major tributaries. The

Commission is now implementing the Master Plan as prepared by Palafox and Associates. The plan calls for development of parks along the riverbanks and designation of environmental protection areas. There are 9 barangays in Pasig lying along the Pasig River.

###### 4. Pasig – Marikina River ChannelImprovement Program

The scope of the Program is the Pasig River upstream to the confluence of the Marikina River and towards Marikina River upstream. Currently in the design stage funded by JICA, the program calls for riverbank development, including dikes/embankment and covers 10 barangays of Pasig.

###### 5. West Manggahan Flood Control

###### Program

This is an OECF funded DPWH project to minimize flooding brought about by Laguna Bay spillage. The plan covers Pasig, Tagig and the adjacent towns of Rizal and involves construction of dikes/embankment on about 2km. stretch of Pasig River in bgy, Pinagbuhatan, Kalawaan, Bambang and San Joaquin, as well as construction of pumping

stations. Subsequently the project defines C6 partially. It is now the implementing stage.

###### Transport Integrated Program)

##### I. DEVELOPMENT TRENDS

1. Increasing commercial

development

a. Increase in the number of services and other commercial establishments per business tax records of the LGU.

Between 1994 and 2000, rate of increase in commercial establishments is almost 100% in accordance with the statistical records of the City Treasurer’s office, compare to low rate of increase of industries which is below 30%.

- b. Rise in pedestrian mall type of shopping centers.

The commercial growth in the last 10 years gave rise to several pedestrian mall typeof shopping centers in Pasig such as the Ever Gostesco Shopping Complex (bgy. Rosario), Sta. Lucia Shopping Mall (bgy. Dela Paz), Pioneer Shopping Center (bgy, Kapitolyo), MAKRO (bgy. Rosario), and the Robinson’s Shopping mall (bgy. Dela Paz). These shopping centers had slowly eased out medium or average sized community grocery stores.

- c. Rapid high rise mixed-use condominium development


As with other Central Business District, high rise mixed-use development has grown in the last 15 years, but has gone rapidly in the last 5 or 6 years, confined at the Ortigas Center and its surrounding areas. The high rise development has spilled over the fringes of the Ortigas Center in bgy, Oranbo, bgy. Ugong and in Barangay San Antonio.

d. Increase in the development of

warehouses

Development of warehouses, mostly for lease has increased in the last 5 years. Some old factory buildings of closed.

Industries were converted to warehouses such as the Old Pimeco plant in bgy. Ugong. From 31 in 1994 the number of Warehouses increased to 158 in 2000.

###### 2. Declining industrial growth

Some old industries have remained in operation, yet have not expanded in the last 20 years in terms of floor area or activities.

Notable among the industries are those located in large areas at the corner of Otigas Avenue and C-5 in bgy. Ugong, at the bend of the Marikina River in bgy. Rosario, at Pasco Avenue in bgy. Santolan, and at Shaw Blvd. in bgy. Kapitolyo. The industries in these areas have shown no

visible signs of expansion nor substantial improvement of physical facilities in the last two (2) decades and these are all located in prime lands

Other industries existing for a longer period of time have ceased operation or relocated to other growth center outside of Metro Manila, perhaps enticed by incentives of the National Government or driven by land and technology requirements. Some of these are the Allied Thread Co., Aris Corporation, Hunter Garments and Marble Craft. Noticeable is the absence of new large or medium size industries in the last ten (10) years. All these purportedly show that industries are becoming less viable or profitable in Pasig .

THE COMPREHENSIVE LAND USE PLAN “ BUILDING A NEW ENVIRONMENT”

###### 3. Residential development

a. Slow subdivision development

Slow residential subdivision development is registered in the last 10 years. There were only about 12 hectares of land that were developed into residential subdivision in the last 10 years, compare to the decades of the 70’s and 80’s. Most of these residential development are all of

medium density type and designed for middle income class group.

b. Increasing density and height

Increasing though not significantly are the development of townhouses and medium rise housing condominiums, although these are more intended for middle income group.

Subdivision residential lots are getting smaller, while nonsubdivision lots are further being subdivided by owners to accommodate growing families.

As density increases, so is the height of single residential structures. We now see three to four storey houses in high intensity nonsubdivision and simple subdivision areas constructed on small plots/lots. In average or middle income subdivision projects of complex type, construction of residences higher

than the usual two-storey structures has stirred up some neighborhood associations and led to various complaints from within. Increasing land values have outdated the present zoning ordinance.

###### 4. New Growth Centers

Development of new growth centers outside Metro Manila will continue to influence the physical environment of the City of Pasig. There are large industries in Pasig that have relocated to CALABARZON growth area. And the MMARILAQUE Region.

On the other hand, the last years had seen the

eastern region, particularly Rizal and the southern region, as a residential expansion of Metro Manila. Improving accessibility of Rizal Province, via Pasig, would further encourage creation of new residential communities in the East.

There are now several average, low, and middle income class residential subdivisions in the East particularly in the towns of Cainta, Taytay, Angono, Antipolo and Binangonan.

##### J. FUNCTIONAL ROLE

- 1. For quite sometime, Pasig has provided

industrial support to the old Metro Manila structure, and as an industrial center, it had aptly performed its role. But changing trends and having a full blown Central Business District with such rapid development and great potentials for expansion, we will allow Pasig to assume the new role of providing trade and commercial support to the metropolis. This new role will enhance the worth and significance of Metro Manila as a potential world class metropolis

- 2. Gateway to the Province of Rizal


This role emanates from the geographical location of Pasig which is at the border of Metro Manila and Rizal Province. Pasig now becomes the major corridor to the East made apparent by the heavy movement of traffic from East to West and vice versa along the major route which is Ortigas Avenue. As such, it is perceived that any physical development in Pasig is strategic to the progress of the province of Rizal.

##### K. CONSTRAINTS

###### 1. High Population growth rate

The NSO date for 1995 reveals an annual growth rate of 3.22% down from 3.99% of the preceding censual period just the same, it is still higher than the national growth rate of 2.2%, although much of this growth rate comes from migration. Based on this growth Pasig will have a doubling population in I8

years. Most visible consequence of heavy migration is the proliferation of squatter settlements and eventually environmental degradation.

###### 2. High Land Valuation

Land values are now up to P8O, OOO per square in the Ortigas vicinity, about P 20,000 on the average in prime lands within the city proper and a minimum of P5, 000 per square meter in other areas.

Land values and taxes as well tremendously increased right after city hood and that was the start of the decline of industrial activities.

###### 3. Elevation of Pasig is 1.00 mts.below mean sea level.

In some cases, invert elevation of drainage system is lower than the sea level. A back flow of waters is being experience in

the poblacion during rainy season when rivers ran high.

###### L. ISSUES AND CONCERNS

###### 1.Incompatible/Conflicting Land Use

a. Some industries are located in residential zones and vice versa. Heavy industries such as Pioneer Ceramics, Apo Chemicals, Gold Medal and Mariwasa are located in designated residential zones/areas. Inversely, Tierra Verde Executive Village, and Carlaville subdivisions in Barangay Santolan are located within industrial zones. Other residential subdivisions such as Bartville, Dreamhomes, Mar Village and Octagon Subdivision were located closely to industrial establishments such as Daichi, Fumakilla, Silahis , Steel Corporation, Bandag and Tierra Stock Pipes in Barangay Dela Paz. In some cases, several batching plants are located very close to high density residential areas.

- b. The existing land use likewise reveals a large block of heavy and pollutive industries along C-5 in Barangay Ugong such as PR Cement, Resins & Ajinomoto among others. Adjacent is the Valle Verde Subdivision, a low density residential zone.
- c. High rise structures are trying to encroach/penetrate middle income class subdivision eliciting adverse reactions from residents thereon. Boundaries where high rise structures are to be permitted without disturbing the peace and serenity of a neighborhood unit are not defined and made clear to the private sector.


###### 2.Loose implementation/absence ofsome development regulations

a. Setback and easement regulations and were openly being violated. There are residential structures that do not reserve open yards, sometimes making

full occupancy of the lot parking requirements of some commercial and institutional establishments are likewise being violated. Multi-storey structures are not equipped with proper open and green spaces. At the Ortigas Center, clustering of high rise structures is constricted that allows less air ventilation. In some cases, height limitation is violated.

- b. Buffer zones as required by zoning

regulations are not strictly enforced on industrial areas.

- c. There are encroachments on sidewalk,

on creeks, and even on right of way or other public open spaces. Even subdivisions encroach on creeks.

- d. No system of enforcement on the part


of LGU (among implementing/regulatory bodies) and between LGU and other implementing agencies. Points or levels of coordination are not defined.

e. There are no stringent rules, particularly during the industrialization period, regarding establishment of industries and their use of the rivers. These industries discharge liquid wastes directly to the rivers with superficial or no treatment at all contributing significantly to water pollution and to the deterioration of the rivers. In some instances, barges owned by these industries are parked permanently alongside of the rivers, used sometimes for storage by the industries or by others. This reduces the width of the rivers.

Easements are likewise being used as yards of the industries. Some have heavy structures built on the easements, such as those located in Barangay Bagong Ilog and Pineda.

###### 3.Squatters

No official count or census has been conducted to determine the accurate number

of squatter families in the entire city, but the initial estimate of 10,000 squatter families appears conservative. These are mostly concentrated on rivers and creeks. Large concentration of squatters is on the banks of Manggahan Floodway and on the unfinished embankment. Tough stand or strong prevention mechanism must be taken by the LGU.

###### 4.The Community Mortgage Program

###### (CMP)

In general, squatter areas are slum or blighted areas. Blighted areas, however are not solely confined to squatter areas since there are tenanted areas that are depressed as well. One solution the government has offered to these areas is the Community Mortgage Program or CMP where squatter families organize themselves into an association to establish a personality to negotiate with the government and the land owners for the acquisition and eventually, subdivisioning of the lot they are occupying disregarding housing standards. The CMP allows and

encourages below acceptable housing standards and this does not contribute to the enhancement of the general housing condition.

Perhaps what ions served by the CMP regularization of land tenure, but the blight situation has remained. While we continue to upgrade regulation for other types of development, grade the standards of low cost housing in Metro Manila just to match the falling affordability level of the program beneficiaries or the maximum loan.

###### 5.Housing Backlog

According to the 1990 NSO Census of Population and Housing, Pasig has a total of 73,119 occupied dwelling units and 77,624 households, 9.6% which are existing in multiple household situation. The Metro Manila Environmental Karte prepared by JICA and Metro Manila Authority in early 90’s reported 19.2% of households as living in unacceptable conditions.

###### 6.Land Related Conflicts/Issues

- a)There are disputes over ownership of large parcels of land that hampers development in Barangay Manggahan and Santolan.
- b) There exist untitled lands, and in addition there are overlapping of titles of serious concern in some areas as in Barangay Santolan
- c) We have various boundary conflicts among Barangay units and with adjacent LGU’s, particularly with the Municipality of Cainta.
- d)Erosion Tax map of the City Assessor’s Office


indicate movement of shoreline of the Pasig River and the Marikina River in Barangay Bambang, Buting, Pinagbuhatan and Barangay Santolan.

e) Squatters in Urban prime land

Large and prime land in Barangay Rosario with great commercial potentials is occupied by squatters who have been staying in the

area for more than 20 years. There were attempts to resolve the problem or evict the squatters thru negotiated or legal means, yet it is feared that with the existing political environment, there might not be a alternative but to sell the property to the settlers themselves, for the lot owners to be compensated. In which case Pasig shall be deprived of a big commercial boost.

###### 7.Traffic Congestion

###### a)Strong traffic flow to the East

The strategies location of Pasig in relation to the Province of Rizal has become the primary source of traffic congestion in the city. And because of this, Pasig will have to bear with its role as a major carrier of traffic to the Eastern Province of Rizal, which had been undergoing a lot of development in recent years. With Ortigas Avenue as the main corridor, Pasig is primarily being used as a travel route between Metro Manila and the East, and the traffic generated out of this relationship appears to be the single biggest factor of

congestion. The forthcoming transfer of the seat of governance of the Province of Rizal to Antipolo will add up to the predominantly Eastern bound traffic yhat runs through the heart of the city. Congestion on Ortigas Avenue has caused traffic to overflow especially during peak hours, to the secondary roads, affecting likewise internal circulation of Pasig.

###### b)Circumferential Road C-5

Aside from the East traffic, Pasig is now being confronted with traffic at Circumferential Road C-5 which runs through 3km. of a busy section of residential and industrial establishments.

C-5 may have relieved EDSA (C-4) some of its traffic load, but it has created a dilemma for Pasig. Since it was opened to fast traffic, it has adversely affected internal mobility in certain parts of the city, in city travel becoming more tedious. In a few years, upon full completion and utilization of C-5 from end to end, more traffic will be drawn into it, and without adequate

infrastructure support it would be difficult to sustain the limited expressway character it was designed to be. Pasig stretch, particularly at Vargas Bridge approach has been identified as one of the bottlenecks of C-5 considering its proximity to the Ortigas Center.

###### c) Street parking

Barangay streets are being used for day and night parking. This is a common occurrence in high density residential areas of the poblacion where carports or off street parking areas are not provided within the residences, mostly in barangays San Miguel, Palatiw, Pinagbuhatan, Sumilang and Bambang.

Ultra, a large recreational institution adjacent to the Ortigas Center create monstrous traffic jams during special events, concerts and games, although occasionally, but nonetheless paralyze tremendously the traffic movement to the Ortigas Center and to the East.

Due to the absence of adequate parking spaces, patrons and clients utilize the streets as parking areas resulting in traffic chaos which extends over a radius of 1.5 km. and congesting the streets and areas of Ortigas Center, C-5, Shaw Blvd. and even the whole stretch of Ortigas Avenue.

Other large educational institutional like the La Immaculada Concepcion which have no parking areas within its property, utilize streets as parking areas and as loading and unloading zones congesting Caruncho Avenue, the main road towards the public market, the City Hall, and the government centers .

Absence or lack of adequate parking spaces in commercial establishments, especially those along commercial strips utilize roads and sidewalks as parking spaces for their clients and their service vehicles. A. Mabini St. located at the city’s poblacion is one strip of commercial structure where heavy traffic occurs due to street parking.

###### d)Proliferation of tricycles

Means of travel frequently used by the residents are the tricycles for inter and intra barangay travel and the jeepneys for traveling to and from neighboring areas of the city. Though tricycles appear to be the most convenient means of travel and though regulated by the barangay units, they posed hazards to pedestrians as they ply their route on narrow roads, made even narrower by street parking.

In addition, there are just too many of them plying overcrowded routes even using road intersections as terminals.

###### e)Laxity in enforcement of traffic rules.

Jeepneys drivers often violate loading and unloading regulations. In some instances jeepney drivers had the temerity to engage each other in conversation in the middle of the road in full view of enforcers. Inaction of enforcers are more of a sign of desperation rather than neglect of duty.

###### f) Excavation

Excavations more often that is not properly coordinated with traffic enforcers. Issuance of excavation permits must be systematized for proper scheduling.

###### 8.Flooding

- a)Apart from the Laguna Bay Spillage Pasig may relatively be free from floods, particularly the town proper, but floods from storm run-off occurs in some areas due to intense rainfall and excessive flows of the Pasig River and Marikina River.

- b)In most cases, storm run-off is due to inadequate, damaged, cut, or clogged lines within the system. As of latest heavy rainfall (October, 1995) there were 94 areas and streets in 20 barangays that experienced flash floods although in low levels due to storm run-off. Flood waters subside mostly in two (2) hours, though.

- c) Excessive flows from Pasig and Marikina Rivers brought about by heavy rainfall


aggravated by absence of dikes/embankment inundate low lying areas along their path in Barangay Pinagbuhatan, Kalawaan, San Joaquin, Bambang, Santolan and Rosario. Flood along low lying areas of the Marikina River also occurs from closure of the Rosario floodgates of the Manggahan Floodway.

d)In some cases, floods remained for days due to blocked or silted esteros inducing a backflow of waters. Portion of some creeks, such as the Hakbangan creek and Pinagkrusan creek have been encroached upon by residential subdivisions, squatters, and industries thereby blocking the flow and reducing the width of the creeks. Pasig and Marikina River, likewise have not been spared of encroachment from squatters. Other creeks located in areas still open for development are drying up like the Sapang Malapit, Sapang Mahabang Ilog and Bambang Liwanag Creek in barangay San Miguel and Maybunga. In some cases, roads have replaced creeks as in Barangay Kalawaan. (Jimenez St.)

e) The most serious floods occurring in Pasig is caused by the Laguna Bay spillage and its occurrences are getting frequent than it used to. This flood which takes months to subside inundate low lying areas surrounding the Pasig River in Barangay Pinagbuhatan, Kalawaan, Bambang, San Joaquin and extends to the nearby barangays of Palatiw, San Miguel and Maybunga. The latest floods of this type occur in year 2000 and in 1998 believed to be aggravated by the flood control mechanism of the Manggahan Floodway structure. These brought to the attention of the national government the adverse effects of the construction and operation of the Manggahan Floodway on the towns of Pasig, Pateros and Tagig.

###### 9.Pollution

a)Establishments of Industries brought along air and water pollution. At present there are about 200 large industries located all over Pasig, some of the heaviest of which are located along the banks of Pasig and Marikina Rivers discharging liquid waste

directly to the river with superficial or no treatment at all. About 54 industries, light to heavy abut the rivers.

Air pollution has been blamed for the rise in respiratory cases among the population in some arts of the city, such as in Barangay Kalawaan, Santolan, and San Joaquin. There are cases when complaints of residents against polluting industries led to closure of these establishments.

The degree of pollution in Pasig does not differ from the rest of Metro Manila towns and cities which is made worse by smoke belching of motor vehicles.

- b)Water pollution does not only come from industries but also from domestic sources. Solid & Liquid waste from households find its way to the river system via the drainage systems.
- c) Much of the noise pollution in Pasig is derived not from industries, but from motorized tricycles plying narrow routes,


disturbing place and tranquility of the neighborhood.

d)Though devolved, Pollution Control functions has not been assumed by the City of Pasig. On the other hand, the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) and the Laguna Lake Development Authority (LLDA) are not exerting any effort to facilitate the devolution, much less to equip local government units with technical capabilities to monitor and implement pollution abatement measures, environmental protection codes, and other related decrees, rules and regulations.

###### 10. Absence of sewer system

Waste water management is through the traditional individual household disposal system. There is not centralized sewerage system not even in urban poor housing projects placed under the Community Mortgage Program. Domestic sewage is disposed off to a septic tank where effluent

is discharge to the storm drain system without any treatment at all.

As a starting point the city and the MWSS must work together in establishing sewer lines and treatment plant in existing housing projects and subdivisions enjoining participation of affected communities. New developments however need to be covered by regulations somewhere, including urban poor housing projects.

###### 10. Absence of constant potable watersupply throughout the day.

a)Through majority of the households are serviced by MWSS, there is no constant supply of potable water in the system throughout the day. Water rationing takes place in most areas particularly within the poblacion and is a source of inconvenience among households. Deep wells have not been a reliable source due to the presence of several factories which draw their water supply from ground water source, resulting in overdraft. There are several areas within Pasig where ground water has almost been

depleted and could no longer be sourced out.

- b)However in spite of water rationing by the MWSS in the poblacion and other parts of the city, some areas enjoy constant MWSS supply throughout the day, such as the Valle Verde subdivision, Barangay Ugong, Manggahan and some parts of Barangay Rosario, In contrast, there are areas where availability of MWSS supply in too limited such as in Barangay San Antonio, Ortigas Center, Barangay Sta. Lucia and Barangay Pinagbuhatan.
- c) Barangay Pinagbuhatan needs to be served immediately, with only 65% of the population being served by MWSS. This is where most depressed communities and urban poor housing projects of the Community Mortgage Program are located, all existing without the benefit of MWSS system. Barangay Manggahan, San Miguel and Palatiw are only 80% being served by MWSS.


d)The age of the system is one area that should be looked into by authorities. Aged systems are usually in the poblacion where rationing takes place and where water levels in the wells continue to decrease, and where most leakages occur.

###### 11. Garbage Disposal

a)Garbage disposal site is one thing that the City Government, and for that matter any single LGU could not afford to maintain, considering, its huge environmental costs. Pasig, just like other LGU’s rely heavily on MMDA for the disposal of garbage and therefore need to fully participate in the preparation of a Metro wide solid waste management plan that include establishment of a sanitary landfill system or other form of garbage disposal and including waste recycling and segregation. While disposal is dependent on MMDA, waste recycling and segregation can be handled independently by the City Government.

###### 12. Burial Site

a)The 3.5 hectare government burial site in Barangay Caniogan and Barangay Santolan and the Roman Catholic Cemetery are already congested. These appears no room for expansion for both, and in a few years with doubling of population in 18 years, cremation may be the only option left.

###### M. LAND REQUIREMENTS

Present land use reveals that only about 15% of the total land area of Pasig is open for development. Due to scarcity of land resources or against demand, it is not expected that each use may be sufficiently served by all requirements particularly in housing where the bulk of the demand is placed. However, other strategies that could offset land requirements can be adopted by the City.

###### 1.Industrial Use

Based on the standard .80 hectare space requirements per 1000 population, the city does not need additional industries until year 2011. But high cost of land makes new industries not viable and likewise forces the existing unproductive industries especially those located on prime land to convert to high yielding commercial uses.

Industrial activity likewise is slowing down as a result of industrial dispersal policy of government and the rise of the new growth centers outside the metropolitan area which have been absorbing established industries away from Pasig. By year 2000 about 3.5% of the total existing areas devoted to industrial use has been converted to commercial use. It is expected/forecasted to drop further by another 3% in the next three (3) years.

Correspondingly, intensified commercial activities is expected to take the frontline in enhancing growth and economy and surpass the economic gains attained by the city during its peak industrial period.

###### 2.Commercial Use

From 1987 to 1988, commercial establishments grew at an average of 7.04% annually. Following this growth rate Pasig will be needing an approximate land area of 454.43 hectares to satisfy its commercial land use requirements for the year 2015 or an additional 300 hectares.

Development at the Ortigas Center, ahs started to spill over the fringes including San Antonio Village, a semi-exclusive residential village in Barangay San Antonio. The rapid pace of development has brought about an increase of about 65% in the number of completed condominium units from 1992 level. The increasing demand of upscale condominium units recognizes the need for opening more areas for commercial high rise development and the need for expanding the scope of the Central Business District. Because of the rise of several business district and other growth centers all over Metro Manila, a broader scope of CBD in Pasig together with better land control and development mechanism

would be needed to increase its competitiveness in business, trade and employment.

Recent trends in the National and Metropolitan levels and increase in land valuation resulting from cityhood and the influence of the Ortigas Center demands a redirection of land use priorities towards high intensity commercialization. The mere presence of the Ortigas Center and its influence on the surrounding areas demand complementary development from the other areas of Pasig that would support or relate to the Central Business District. With declining industries, intensifying commercializing would be the best option for maintaining the economic progress achieved by the city.

###### 3.Residential Use

The city’s housing requirements in 20 years have gone beyond the resources of the local government unit in terms of land resources and finance.

A total of 28.8% of households in 1990 has been reported in the Metro Manila Urban Area Environment Karte as in need of housing units. The same trend increases the demand to 32,869 units for the year 2000 and to 42,820 units in 2008. For a density of 100 units per hectare, additional land requirements for housing would reach 426 hectares in the year 2008.

###### 4.Parks

Base on subdivision standards, additional land requirements for park for the present nee is 32.50 hectares and 6.79 hectares for future requirements approximating a total of 40 hectares.

###### D. KEY DEVELOPMENT AREAS

###### a. BARANGAY KAPITOLYO

Going by the present trends in development, the present use of a large portion of Barangay Kapitolyo

(industrial block) had not been fully maximized. These areas possess great potentials for redevelopment considering its geographical location, size and capability. Adjacent to it is the Central Business District as proposed by the City of Mandaluyong. Areas for integration/coordination with Mandaluyong must be identified.

###### b.BARANGAY SANTOLAN

a) The industrial block bounded by Pasco Ave., Marikina River, Marcos Highway and Among Rodriguez possess a great potential for redevelopment. Aside from its physical characteristic, the CBD proposal of Marikina across Marcos Highway could complement its development.

A new type of development in Barangay Santolan will enliven Barangay Santolan and its people which until now had stagnated for quite a long period of time despite the presence of industries and even during the height of industrial development.

The LRT project could catalyze the transformation that could also be an opportunity to develop integrally the Marikina River System.

b) A large portion of barangay Santolan along the riverbanks are occupied by informal settlers on land whose ownership has been a subject of dispute for a long period of time. The ownership issue has dragged for decades and has set back all development efforts. In order to stabilize the community, the government could intervene to resolve the land issue in favor of the occupants who have been staying in the area for not less than 30 years and preposition a 20 meter special easement zone along Marikina River.

###### 3. UPPER MARIKINA RIVER AREAS

The large parcels of land occupied presently by heavy and medium industries in Manggahan, Rosario and Ugong along Marikina River are possibilities for large scale redevelopment. Aside from being perceived as unproductive showing no visible signs of expansion, the presence of these industries has not stimulated the growth of other activities in the Barangay.

###### 4. BARANGAY UGONG

The high land valuation in barangay Ugong could spur another process of development. Riped for a new type of development, the industrial block along C-5 should be given the though of minimizing it conflict with adjacent land use (Valle Verde subdivision) and complementing the Ortigas Commercial Center. This area is being left out of the development of Barangay Ugong.

###### 5. THE ORGITAS PROPERTY, C-5,BARANGAY UGONG

This is a 15 hectare vacant property with a very high land valuation located

on an environment that is essentially residential and low density. Its high commercial value amidst a residential environment might cause a conflict in land use development and could cause a concern for would be developers.

###### 6. RIZAL PROVINCIAL CAPITOL

This area will soon be vacated by the province of Rizal upon relocation of its seat of government to Antipolo. It reverted back to the private sector, Pasig could loose a great source of wide open spaces that serve as breathing spell in an environment of high rise and constricted structures and with the highest land valuation among Pasig Areas. The LGU could exercise its power to reclassify this area into some uses that would redound to the welfare and benefits of its people and produce a positive impact on the environment.

###### 7. MARKET VICINITY

The absence of a semblance of order at the way development is taking place in the commercial areas of the market vicinity demands a reinforcement in development regulations, Setbacks, easement, green spaces, use of arcades & standard design of pedestrian facilities are some of the areas that should be covered by strong and proper regulations. At present, there seems to be a random type of development taking place.

###### 8. THE OLD TOWN PLAZA

With evidences of history, development of this area could be the starting point in heritage development. The historical enclave could extend beyond its boundaries to cover areas of Barangay Malinao, San Jose and San Nicolas.

###### 9. BARANGAY KALAWAAN,PINAGBUHATAN AND SAN JOAQUIN

These 3 barangays need the most government attention in terms of enhancing the environment, be it in terms of land, use housing or flood

control. The presence of some industrial activities in these barangays has not contributed to a healthy environment, much more the general growth of the barangay and its residents. Housing appears to be the most important component in environmental development of these areas considering the large number and the length of stay of the informal settlers, and in the case of barangay Pinagbuhatan, the number of poor families who were resettled in the area through Community Mortgage Program.

Barangay Pinagbuhatan has become the favorite location of squatters and urban poor housing projects due to low cost of land. Focus of attention should be on areas near the old dumpsite and the closest to Laguna de Bay.

The riverbanks of these 3 barangays which are all located along the Pasig River could be taken

advantage of source of parks, playground, and open spaces for highly congested areas, in which case easements could be made wider.

###### 10. MANGGAHAN FLOODWAY

The stretch of Manggahan Floodway from Ortigas southward covered by Barangay Maybunga, San Miguel and Sta. Lucia has become as eyesore. It is now occupied by thousand of squatters mostly settled on the embankment which ahs remained unfurnished up to this time. Completion of the embankment and the east and west bank road which is tantamount to removal of the squatters is the biggest task ahead for the LGU, the DPWH and the NHA. The project needs to be completed not only for visual purposes but for full utilization of the project.

###### 11. BARANGAY STA. ROSA

Almost whole of population of barangay Sta. Rosa are tenants of Rizal Provincial Government. The main issue is

securitization of land tenure. However, the area occupied by the population is only one-third of the land area of the barangay. The other part of the barangay is occupied by a warehouse of an industrial establishment and the old Capitol building of the Rizal Provincial Government with wide open spaces which maybe made available for parks and playground. This barangay located at the cross of Pasig River and Marikina River comprise only of 8 hectares. With land security problems and some structure to be affected by the easement zone of the rivers, studies should be made on how it could be united and incorporated with the contiguous small barangays of Bagong Katipunan and Sumilang.

###### 12. OLD CAPITOL SITE, BARANGAY STA. ROSA

The structure that used to house the seat of Rizal Provincial Government presents a good view

from C-5 – Pasig River bridge. The building with all of its historical value must be preserved for some special uses and enhanced by parks and promenade extending to the site owned by the DPWH.

###### 13. BARANGAY PALATIW, BAMBANGAND SAN MIGUEL

This NHA developed housing projects consists of about 80 hectares of homelots for low income families. Development in such a huge size of subdivision with individually constructed housing units could influence the environment. Though the NHA is properly regulating the development, the project needs a lot of support from the City Government especially in so far as maintaining roads and drainage system is concerned.

###### 14. KARANGALAN SITES ANDSERVICES

This NHA developed housing projects consists of about 80 hectares of

Traffic along C-5 in Pasig, as in Ortigas Avenue, will largely affect the internal circulation within Pasig hence

This road used to be one of the oldest centers of trade and commerce in Pasig but deteriorating traffic and road conditions have caused a slowdown of development activities and failed to attract big investors not with standing its great potential for an increased level of development.

###### 20. SANDOVAL AVENUE

This road has at present become the primary access to the urban poor housing projects located not only in the territorial jurisdiction of Pasig, but also in the boundaries such as Taytay and Tagig.

Though least developed being low lying and close to Laguna de Bay, the low land values had made these areas become the favorite location for high density urban poor housing projects and relocation centers particularly those under the Community Mortgage Program.

Such case of development going ahead of the infrastructure, nevertheless, has made it imperative for the local government to respond and develop plans to meet the needs and requirements of these areas. Upgrading of this road is in order and should involve expansion in length and in with and for greeneries. Amidst a highly densed condition around this road is an opportunity to create green and open spaces. industrial development and its future linkages to C-6 and the Laguna Bay area.

###### F. AREAS OF STUDY

1. Barangay Consolidation/Segregatin

There exist a wide variance between the smallest and largest barangays. Small barangays are as low as 6 hectares such as St. Rosa, Sta. Cruz, Sto. Tomas and San Jose, while large barangays could go as high as 375 hectare, such as Mangghan, Rosario, Ugong and Manggahan.

Redefining Barangay boundaries could improve governance and implementation of programs and projects. Small barangays are expensive to manage and maintain on the part of the city government. On the part of these barangay units, standing on its size and small income could hardly allow them to decentralize from the city government. On the other hand, large barangays experience breakdown in services such as infrastructure, livelihood and management services.

Another issue is the present boundaries by which barangays are defined. Most of the barangays are defined by arbitrary lines and not by natural boundaries which lead to various conflicts between adjacent barangay units.

As an initial input to the study, the following Barangay units could integrate:

- a)Sta. Rosa and Sumilang
- b)Barangya Katipunan, San Jose and portion of Bambang
- c) San Nicolas, Sta. Cruz, and portion of Sto. Tomas
- d)Sagad and portion of San Miguel


###### 2. Solid Waste Disposal

Sooner or later, every LGU in Metro Manila will have to face its own disposal problem, independent of the Metro Manila Development Authority. By recent development viz a viz, the nonavailability of dumping site, the city government should make a through study on the proper method and technology in garbage disposal applicable to the city, taking into

considerations the constraints/provision of existing laws, the environmental cost of maintenance, economic use, and financial capability of the LGU. Studies should also focus on joint operation with other willing local government units.

Consideration of Solid Waste Management Act (R.A. 9003) shall be taken particularly with regards to provision of other solid waste reduction facilities.

###### 3. Rainforest Extensions/Expansion

Studies can be made the feasibility of extending the rainforest by creeps of linear pocket parks towards residential areas, especially those that are densely populated. Studies could include the possibility of accessing the Central Park via creeping pocket parks by foot or by bicycles.

For access and expansion, the Rainforest could make use of several surrounding creeks that mostly lead to populated areas.

4. Another Public market, via a viz, the

Rosario Talipapa.

At present, the residents of District II, are being served by a privately owned talipapa located at the corner of Ortigas Avenue and Dr. Sixto Antonio Avenue in a highly congested area of Barangay Rosario.

The present location of this talipapa poses a threat to the safety and convenience of market patrons, pedestrians, motorist and the ambulant vendors who already occupy portions of the street in the course of their peddling. The talipapa which occupies a small area squeezed by commercial buildings contribute largely to the congestion at the Ortigas intersection and to the environmental degradation of Barangay Rosario.

A study on the development of a new public market to serve residents of the District II should consider the closure of this talipapa and transfer all its market

activities to the proposed public market. The present Mutya ng Pasig Market at the Caruncho Complex badly needs to be decongested. Mere expansion of the public market in its present site may not be enough to serve its expanding clientele which comes not only from within Pasig but also from the neighboring towns of Tagig, Pateros and the Rizal Province, considering the capacity of the existing facilities to absorb development expansion. The proposed public market would decongest the existing public market in terms of clientele/patrons, ambulant vendors and overcrowded stalls and eventually improve its operation and maintenance.

5. Revolving Tower

Without the revolving tower restaurant on the topmost (12th) floor, it is believed that this building has lost its landmark interest and may have been used in appropriately not because of management lapses but due to high cost maintenance.

Through the years, it may seem to have lost its high commercial value that initially merited a revolving restaurant due to the prevailing environment. Nevertheless, this edifice towering over the immediate surrounding could be used appropriately for other developmental purposes that could complement the character of the environment, that could earn Pasig a distinction of some sort, possibly in support of tourism or cultural development of the city.

###### 2. Population Program

Although the main objective of the program is to reduce growth rate, it should not only concentrate on population control, but also in assisting the population in accessing and in participating in governments program and services. More importantly, however is concentration on reproductive

health and checking migration to which the increase in the population of Pasig is attributed.

###### 3. Tourism Program

This program should come up with an inventory of tourist potentials and spot and adopt measures to increase the tourist interest of the city.

The program should involve among others.

- a) Restoration of existing historical landmarks.
- b) Identification of new/potential tourist sites
- c) Development of tourism related livelihood projects
- d) Intensive beautification campaign and activities
- e) Development of a center for cultural activities and presentation.


###### 4. Cooperative Development Program

The program should lead to establishment of multipurpose cooperatives in every barangay and eventually, establishment of cooperative communities. It is expected cooperative communities could assist the local government

unit in the development and implementation of programs and projects, particularly those related solid waste and housing.
