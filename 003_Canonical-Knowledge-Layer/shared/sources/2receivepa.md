###### EXPLANATORY NOTE

“A business without a sign is a sign of no business.”

The foregoing lines in a billboard being offered for rent somewhere along the periphery

of the North Luzon Expressway caught our attention and our fascination thereof remains to

these days.

Indeed, a good ad lingers in peoples’ minds; its recall value buoys a person into patronage and purchase of a product or service. It came as no surprise, therefore, that billboards grew like mushrooms all over the metropolis and in strategic areas because of the effectiveness in capturing the attention of the intended market, as well as for its cost-efficiency

as a medium of advertising vis-é-vis traditional media.

The laws regulating out-of-home media (OOH), billboards included, are scattered

elsewhere in our statute books — in the outdated Building Code ofthe Philippines of 1972 (R.A.

No. 6541), Chapter 20 of the National Building Code of the Philippines (Presidential Decree No.

1096) and its detailed Implementing Rules and Regulations, the Philippine Electrical Code, the Code of Ethics for Advertising and Promotions, the Philippine Highway Act of 1953 (Republic Act No. 917), the National Structural Code of the Philippines of 1992, some relevant Civil Code

provisions on easement and nuisance, as well as in various Ordinances of local government units.

‘The bedlam in the present set-up lies in the absence of an exacting law on OOH media.

And where different municipalities, cities, and entities are imposing different rates of regulatory fees, there is indeed a need for a uniform schedule of applicable fees.

Safety standards is also one important component of this proposed legislative measure.

Spacing, height, size, location, strength of materials, etc. are but some of the vital regulations

thisbillseeksto impose.

Other than a recognition of the value of outdoor out-of-home media as an effective

marketing tool, this proposed piece of legislation also aims to address the industry’s clamor for

SECTION 11. Penal Clause. - It shall be unlawful for any natural or juridical person to directly or indirectly erect, construct, enlarge, repair, move, remove or maintain any outdoor media contrary to or in violation of any provision of this Act.

No officer or employee of the national or local governments, now or hereafter

charged with the enforcement of laws, ordinances or regulations relating to the

construction, erection or alteration of outdoor media, shall endorse the approval of any

plans, designs or specifications which have not been prepared and submitted in strict and full accord with all the provisions of this Act.

Any individual or firm, whether in the private or public sector, who shall violate any

of the provisions of this Act shall, upon conviction by a court of competent jurisdiction, be punished by a fine of not less than thrice the amount of the outdoor medium including its support structure, but not more than ten times the amount of said outdoor medium including its support structure, or by imprisonment of not less than one (1) year but not

more than five (5) years, or both, at the discretion of the court. In case of a firm,

partnership, corporation or association, whether private or public, the penalty shall be imposed upon its responsible officers for such violation. In case the guilty party is an alien, he/she shall immediately be deported after payment of the fine and/or service of sentence.
