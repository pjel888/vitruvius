3.8 OVER-CURRENT CIRCUIT PROTECTIVE DEVICES - are devices whose sole purpose is to protect insulation, wiring, switches and other apparatus from overheating or burning, due to

3.8.1 FUSE’ —is a device consisting of an alloy link of wire

with a low melting temperature which is inserted in the

circuit, in such a way, that all current which passes through

the circuit, must also pass through this metal. Any current which would be dangerous to the circuit melts this fuse,

opens the circuit at this point and thus protects the rest of

the components from the effects of the current. The cause

of the large current may then be removed and a new fuse

inserted in place of the broken one.

When the glare is caused by light sources in the field of vision, it is known as “direct

glare” or “disability glare”. When glare is caused by the reflection of a light source in a

viewed surface, it is known as “reflected glare” or “discomfort glare”.

Bare lamps or brilliant fixture globes should never be in the line of sight from any point in the room. An angle of 45 deg. between the horizontal and the line of sight (line from the lampto the eye) is generally accepted as the greatest permissible angle.

daylight to bluish white and even pinkish white. Color is also used to enhance certain qualities. For example, deluxe FL lamps are used to enhance food in restaurants.

2. THENATUREOFFIRE

Fire is defined as the phenomenon of combustion manifested in light, flame and heat (Webster). There are three elements which are needed to start and to sustain a fire. These are heat energy,

which provides the ignition; fuel, which make up the fire load; and oxygen which provides the

ventilation. The principle of preventing a fire from starting and suppressing a fire is based on the fact that if any one of these elements is absent and/or is controlled, the process of oxidation can not be

started nor sustained.

Therearefourclassesoffireaccordingtosource:

- 1) CLASSA fires involve ordinary combustible materials such as wood, cloth, paper, rubber, plastics andthelike.
- 2) CLASSB fires involve flammable and combustible liquids and flammable gases.
- 3) CLASSC fires involve electrical wiring, cables and equipment.
- 4) CLASSD fires involve combustible metals such as potassium, sodium, magnesium and other reactivemetals.

Fire growth follows a logical progression of stages. In the first stage, a fire usually starts from a small source such as igniting the contents of a waste basket by means of a disposed lighted cigarette butt.

The local contents burn and ignite additional combustible materials nearby such as a curtain near the

waste basket. This, in tum, produces more heat thereby increasing the rate at which the fire develops.

The fire spreads in an upward direction and heats the wood ceiling boards and engulfs the room. This large fuel load, during the second stage, decomposes with the evolution of a flammable gas mixture, the ignition of which cduses an even explosive rate of fire spread. The third stage involves a fully developed fire which penetrates through doors, windows, ceiling, and walls and spreads towards the adjacent areas until the whole building is in flames. The fourth stage involves the decay stage of a fire

when the fuel load is already expended.
