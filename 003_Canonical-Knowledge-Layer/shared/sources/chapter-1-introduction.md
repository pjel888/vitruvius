# CHAPTER 1 INTRODUCTION

This Chapter explains in a common manner how a Comprehensive Land Use Plan (CLUP) is prepared, at the same time the approaches and methodologies used to prepare a particular CLUP for the municipality of Palauig. Palauig being situated within the Province of Zambales is thereby affected by regional developments and national policies, thus linkages of the plan with the provincial, regional and national developments will also be discussed. Plus, the relative proximity of Palauig to certain nationally significant investment centers and development areas necessitate mentioning some relevant national plans.

# 1.1 Overview and Rationale of Physical Planning

Physical planning involves the determining of general land use pattern, the location and character of the built environment in order to promote economic betterment, comfort, convenience and people’s general welfare (Webster, 1958). Intensive physical and structure planning has to be done in order to guide the allocation, utilization, development and management of resources through sectoral plans and policies that will guide and regulate growth and development in an area. Structure planning is among the approaches to physical development planning that provides actual delineation of boundaries or zones within the territorial jurisdiction in order to embody the desired pattern of land use which will be translated into sectoral plans and policies (HLURB, 2013).

# 1.2 Legal and Enabling Policy Environment

The preparation of a physical plan like the Comprehensive Land Use Plan of Palauig, is a constitutionally mandated activity to all local government units. The authority in the preparation of these plans particularly, the Comprehensive Land Use Plan (CLUP) emanates from the provision of the 1987 Constitution that provides for the sustainable management and development of the country’s resources. Specifically, the Constitution mandates the following:

“The Congress shall give highest priority to the enactment of measures that protect and enhance the right of all the people to human dignity, reduce social, economic, and political inequalities, and remove cultural inequities by equitably diffusing wealth and political power for the common good. To this end, the State shall regulate the acquisition, ownership, use and disposition of property and its increments”

- Article XIII, Section 1

“The use of property bears a social function and all economic agents shall contribute to the common good. Individuals and private groups, including corporations, cooperatives, and similar collective organizations, shall have the right to own, establish and operate economic enterprises, subject to the duty of the State to promote distributive justice and to intervene when the common good so demands”

- Article XII, Section 6

The primary responsibility over physical planning is mandated to the local government units (LGUs) especially when most governmental functions were devolved to the LGUs upon enactment of the Local Government Code of 1991 (Republic Act 7160) which, among others, provides the following:

“Every LGU shall exercise the powers expressly granted, those necessarily implied there from, as well as powers necessary, appropriate or incidental for its efficient and effective governance, and those which are essential for the promotion of the general welfare. Within their respective territorial jurisdictions, local government units shall ensure and support, among other things, the preservation and enrichment of culture, promote health and safety, enhance the right of the people to a balanced ecology, encourage and support the development of appropriate and self-reliant scientific and technological capabilities, improve public morals, enhance economic prosperity and social justice, promote full employment among their residents, maintain peace and order and preserve the comfort and convenience of their inhabitants.”

– Section 16 “The local government units shall, in conformity with existing law, continue to prepare their respective Comprehensive Land Use Plans enacted through zoning ordinances which shall be the primary and dominant bases for the future use of the land resources”

– Section 20 (c)

“Adopt a Comprehensive Land Use Plan for the municipality/city: Provided that the formulation, adoption, or modification of said plan shall be in coordination with the approved Provincial Physical Plan.”

– Sections 447(2)(vii) and 458(2)(vii)

“Enact integrated zoning ordinances in consonance with the approved Comprehensive Land Use Plan, subject to existing laws, rules and regulations...”

– Sections 447(2)(ix) and 458(2)(ix)

“Prescribe reasonable limits and restraints on the use of property within the jurisdiction of the municipality/city”

– Sections 447(a)(2)(vi) and 458(a)(2)(vi)

“Adopt measures to safeguard and conserve land, mineral, marine, forest, and other resources of the municipality/city”

– Sections 444(b)(3)(vii) and 455(b)(3)(vii)

“(vii) Review the Comprehensive Land Use Plans and zoning ordinances of component cities and municipalities and adopt a Comprehensive Provincial Physical plan, subject to existing laws.”

– Section 468, (2), Article III, Chapter 3, Book III

In order to enjoin all LGUs to prepare and implement their CLUPs, the review and approval of the CLUP was mandated to the Housing and Land Use Regulatory Board and the Sangguniang Panlalawigan through the Provincial Land Use Committee. Executive Order Number 72 enumerated and delineated these functions, as follows:

“(a) Cities and municipalities shall continue to prepare or update their Comprehensive Land Use Plans, in conformity with the land use planning standards and guidelines prescribed by the HLURB and to national policies.”

“(c) Cities and municipalities of Metro Manila shall continue to formulate or update their respective land use plans, in conformity with the land use planning and zoning standards and guidelines prescribed by HLURB.”

- “(d) ... the powers of the HLURB to review and approve the Comprehensive Land Use Plans of component cities and municipalities are hereby devolved to the province.”
- “(e) Pursuant to LOI 729, S. of 1978, EO 648, S. of 1981 and RA 7279, the Comprehensive Land Use Plans of provinces, highly-urbanized cities and independent component cities shall be reviewed and ratified by the HLURB to ensure compliance with national standards and guidelines.”

- “(f) Pursuant to EO 392 S. of 1990, the Comprehensive Land Use Plans of cities and municipalities of Metropolitan Manila shall be reviewed by HLURB to ensure compliance with national standards and guidelines.”

- Sections 1(a, c) and 2 (a, e, f)

The importance of the preparation of the land use plan was further highlighted when the Urban Development and Housing Act (RA 7279) was signed into law, providing a comprehensive definition of a land use plan as a “rationale approach of allocating available land resources as equitably as possible among competing user groups and for different functions consistent with the development plan of the area and the program under this Act” (Section 3(k)).

The recent enactment of two landmark legislations provided LGUs with a more critical role in physical development planning. The Climate Change Act of 2009 (RA 9729) and the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010 (RA 10121) provided the integration of climate change action plans and disaster risk reduction management plans in various local plans including the CLUP and the Comprehensive Development Plan. Other related laws that concern the management and conservation of local resources include the following, among others:

- • Republic Act 7586 – National Integrated Protected Areas System Act
- • Republic Act 8435 – Agricultural Fisheries Modernization Act
- • Republic Act 8550 – Revised Fisheries Code (Revised)
- • Republic Act 9003 – Ecological Solid Waste Management Act
- • Republic Act 9593 – National Tourism Act
- • Republic Act 10066 – National Cultural Heritage Act
- • Republic Act 9593 - National Tourism Act of 2009
- • Commonwealth Act 141 - Public Land Act
- • Presidential Decree 1076 - Water Code of the Philippines

The Plan is guided by the frameworks and strategies embodied by existing national plans and programs to ensure integration and contribution to the national development agenda. Among them is Philippine Agenda 21 (PA21) which was formulated in 1996 and focused on sustainable development strategies to achieve better quality of life for all Filipinos. The five

goal elements of PA21 include poverty reduction, social equity, empowerment and social governance, peace and solidarity, and ecological integrity.

In compliance with national initiatives to mainstream climate change and disaster risk reduction and management, the salient provisions of the National Strategic Framework for Climate Change and the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Plan will, as much as practicable, be integrated.

# 1.3.1 National Physical Framework Plan (NPFP)

The National Framework for Physical Planning (NFPP) 2001 to 2030 provides a broad long-term development vision based on the physical characteristics suited to the Philippines. With this, its vision and goals focus on the following principles : (1) food security, (2) environmental stability and ecological integrity, (3) rational urban development, (4) spatial integration, (5) equitable access to physical and natural resources, (6) private-public sector partnership, (6) people empowerment, (7) recognition of the rights of indigenous people and (8) market orientation.

The NFPP also assumes that the overall physical planning challenge is to provide policy guidelines towards the envisioned growth of the country in a manner that is efficient, equitable, and sustainable. The basic concern that the country faces is that of limited physical and economic resources and increasing demands from a growing population. It has particularly stated that policy guidelines need to address the following specific challenges: increasing urban population, density, and demand for urban services; unplanned expansion of settlement areas; declining agricultural productivity; land degradation; limited access to land; outdated land use plans and the increasing role of local government units in planning; and lack of institutional linkages

The NPFP was a response to the presidential directive to formulate an integrated national land use policy agenda that would guide the allocation, utilization, development and management of the country’s physical resources. However, with the emergence of new laws, sectoral plans and policies, the National Land Use Committee recognized the urgency of refining and updating the NPFP in order to make it more responsive in guiding decisions on the use, allocation, management and development of land resources. As a result, a National Framework for Physical Planning (NFPP) was deemed more appropriate and relevant as a planning document. The NFPP provides the analytical parameters for the planned allocation, use and management of the country’s land and other physical resources. It is intended to serve as a framework through which the planning and management of these resources are guided at the national and sub-national levels. Both the NPFP and NFPP serve as general guides in the formulation of a CLUP.

# 1.3.2 Regional Physical Framework Plan (RPFP)

The RPFP serves as guide to decisions on how land and natural resources may be put to the most beneficial use for the people. It indicates how resources may be managed and conserved for the benefit of present and future generations. It is designed to show the

desired direction and amount of growth of the region as a whole and its sub-regional areas over a plan period of thirty (30) years. It intends to promote the direction and amount of growth that represents a balance between the need for the region to fulfil its functional role in the national economy and the need to ensure sustainable utilization of physical resources. Thus, it takes into account and seeks to contribute to the attainment of national development goals and targets. It also seeks to guide public and private investment to achieve an efficient settlement pattern providing better access by the region’s population to basic services. It seeks a rational mix of land uses that promote both productivity and environmental integrity.

Further detailing the direction of the policies and guidelines of the CLUP of the municipality of Palauig is its alignment with the Central Luzon Regional Physical Framework Plan (RPFP) 20052030. The current vision of the said RPFP is:

“To have globally competitive human resources, a highly productive and profitable agricultural sector, self-propelling LGUs ably supported by the national government, seamless and integrated physical access and, a transshipment and logistics hub in the Asia-Pacific Region, a favored international convention center and tourist destination, a developed industrial heartland in Southeast Asia and a model of sustainable utilization and management of forest and mineral resources”

The CLUP of Palauig will further detail vision and policies as aligned in both the NFPP and RPFP. In particular, in accordance to the NFPP and RPFP, the structure plan and its corresponding policy guidelines would have a framework addressing the following specific challenges: increasing urban population, density, and demand for urban services; unplanned expansion of settlement areas; declining agricultural productivity; land degradation; limited access to land; outdated land use plans and the increasing role of local government units in planning; and lack of institutional linkages.

# 1.3.3 Relevant Regional Plan

The W-Growth Corridor defines Central Luzon’s key growth areas. It is a strategic approach in promoting Central Luzon as an investment destination. It comprises Central Luzon key investment areas for tourism, industry and agriculture. These areas represent the growth municipalities of the region, which when plotted on a map form the shape of a W.

The Central Luzon W-Growth Corridor concept was derived from the region’s articulated vision of becoming:

- ▪ An Industrial Heartland of the Philippines and the Asia-Pacific Region;
- ▪ An International Transshipment Hub;
- ▪ A World Conference Center; and,
- ▪ A Showcase of Competitive and Vibrant Agriculture Sector.

The municipality of Palauig being part and parcel of Zambales can benefit from activities taking place within the Clark Special Economic Zone by identifying forward and backward linkages that can support activities in this area. Road interchanges within Zambales may be proposed to provide greater accessibility to areas in the vicinity of the Subic-Clark Alliance for Development Program (SCAD).

The third leg of the W-Growth Corridor, the so-called Green Corridor, is designed to encourage the development of farms devoted to high value crops and agri-forestry. The vast farmlands of the province which occupy 203,985 hectares or 66.8 percent of the province’s land area can readily meet this challenge. The existing road network that link interior barangays to the growth centers of the province can be used to transport farm produce to identified markets.

# 1.3.4 The Subic Clark Alliance Development Zone (SCADZ)

The Subic Clark Alliance Development Zone or SCADZ is envisioned as a world-class Mega Logistics Hub that can provide smooth delivery of goods, services, people and information to and from the production, manufacturing, and trading centers of the country. It is also planned to serve the Asia-Pacific Supply Chain.

The Subic-Clark Alliance for Development Council (SCADC) has completed the Conceptual Land-Use Plan (CLUP) for the Subic-Clark Economic Corridor. The SCoLUP, which was started in September 2008, covers approximately a ten (10) kilometer-wide swath with the SubicClark-Tarlac Expressway at its center line. It runs the entire length of the SCTEX and is comprised of productive/non-productive agricultural lands, minor forests and built-up areas. All in all, it covers an approximately one hundred thousand (100,000) hectares, located in four (4) provinces, including Zambales ten (10) municipalities and three other (3) cities being traversed by the SCTEX.

# 1.3.5 Luzon Urban Beltway (LUB)

The Luzon Urban Beltway (LUB) is envisioned as a seamless, investment haven that will attract more businesses to the area. This development links South Luzon as far as Batangas to Northern Luzon through the Star Tollways system-South Luzon Expressway-SLEX-C5-NLEX in an unbroken expressway route. This Beltway can pave the way in terms of access for more tourists to Zambales from the southern region.

# 1.3.6 The Provincial Development and Physical Plan

The provincial framework is founded on areas and factors where Zambales enjoys natural competitive advantages. Planners had to locate Zambales’ development within the context of North Luzon’s focus as an agribusiness center, Metro Luzon’s emerging role as the Urban Beltway, and Central Luzon’s potential as a tourism hub. To be anticipated is the political transformation toward less dependency on central government and conversely, more authority and devolution of powers to local governments (PDPFP, 2014).

The above context will shift planned investment programs and projects of the province toward: more land transport access, increase in the level of service of strategic roads, integration of land, air, sea transport modes, upgrading of tourism establishments, and the improvement of Clark and Subic as tourism gateways. At the same time, the Municipality of Palauig will assume greater supervision of micro, small and medium enterprises to ensure that these are relevant to the demands of the external environment and the national government’s vision of inclusive growth.

The PDPFP envisions Zambales as:

Zambales 2025: A developing province ready to take a leadership role in the economic, political, socio cultural, and environmental affairs of the nation and whose progress is driven by:

- ▪ High yields in technology-aided agriculture and fisheries;
- ▪ Globally competitive sports- and eco-tourism
- ▪ Successful mining and industrial ventures
- ▪ Easily accessible and environmentally protected locations
- ▪ Expanding urbanized centers and crime-free municipalities
- ▪ Efficient public service, and

- ▪ A warm, hospitable people reaping the benefits of sustainable development under a responsive political leadership.

# 1.4 Approach and Methodology

This is an updating and revision of the CLUP of the municipality of Palauig. The plan is intended to take into account recent developments and policies. In addition to these, perceived gaps within the existing CLUP and its implementation are also attempted to be addressed. Through coordination by the Municipal Planning and Development Office and the Municipality of Palauig stakeholders, the said updating and revision are made under way.

Facilitation of the process is headed by two technical assistants, and the final approval, validation, and adoption of this plan belong to the various stakeholders that included the political leaders, members of the business sector, religious groups, non-governmental organizations, and the civil society, among others.

Vision-setting meetings were conducted to ensure ownership and participation by the various stakeholders of the new vision for Palauig.

Data were gathered from the national government agencies concerned such as the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), Department of Agriculture (DA), and National Mapping Resources Information Authority (NAMRIA). Several interviews from both municipal and provincial planning officials were conducted. Site visits and validation were also undertaken to get information on existing land uses and assess economic development in the respective areas. These were done to ensure comprehensiveness of the plan to cover all geographical locations.

After several months of facilitating consultative meetings, site visits, data gathering, and discussions by the stakeholders, this draft Comprehensive Land Use Plan mainstreaming of Climate and Disaster Risk Assessment (CDRA) in the plan document, will be given to the municipal planner and leaders for further consideration and eventual approval. A draft Zoning Ordinance shall also be prepared to be validated by the MPDO.

The updating and revision process generally conformed to the following procedure of Local Comprehensive Planning Process existing in literature and being put into practice by LGUs in the country, among them are the following:

Inclusive and expansive governance. This involves the active participation of the three actors in governance – the state, the civil society and the private sector. The collaboration of these actors will lead to a plan built upon the foundations of good governance, one that is participatory, accountable, responsible, transparent and sustainable.

Co-management principle. This includes responsibility-sharing between the national and the local governments in terms of ecological balance management and maintenance pursuant to the provisions of the Local Government Code and advance the welfare of the national territory and patrimony.

Bottom-up Approach. The barangay-level plans and profile are integrated to the city-level plan in order to harmonize the development goals and objectives of the city and the barangays. This will also lead to the identification and reconciliation of inconsistent and incompatible plans among barangays.

Top-to-Bottom Approach. The national and sub-national policies and frameworks mentioned earlier are likewise considered as basis and reference for strategic development options and alternatives for the structural plan.

# 1.5. Planning Area and Period

The planning area constitutes the administrative and political boundaries of Palauig in Zambales, particularly the component barangays that delineate the planning area. The planning period of more or less nine years from 2015 to 2024 shall be adopted for the municipality since Palauig taking into consideration the pace of development of the municipality.

- 1.6. Expected Outputs

- 1.6.1. Comprehensive Land Use Plan.

This CLUP for the Municipality of Palauig, Zambales will serve as the primary basis for determining the future use of land and other natural resources within the Municipality, subject to national laws and policies. The CLUP shall also serve as the basis for prescribing reasonable limits and restraints on the use of property, for regulating subdivision developments, and for reclassifying agricultural lands into non-agricultural uses. Being comprehensive in geographical scope, the CLUP covers the entire territorial jurisdiction of Palauig including those areas that are traditionally the domain of the national government. The authority to plan and manage these latter areas shall now be shared between the LGU and the national government. To adequately cover every part of the territorial jurisdiction of the city, the CLUP embodies appropriate policies for each of the land use policy areas. Moreover, the CLUP is a long-term policy guide that spans several terms of local officials so that continuity of development programs is ensured.

# 1.4.2 Zoning Ordinance.

The principal instrument for enforcing the locational policies and performance standards of the CLUP is the zoning ordinance. Unless the CLUP is enacted into a zoning ordinance it remains an indicative plan with only persuasive force and effect. Once the zoning ordinance is enacted, the right of property owners to develop their lands will be regulated by society through appropriate permits and clearances from the municipal government.

# CHAPTER 2 MUNICIPAL VISION AND THE SPATIAL DEVELOPMENT FRAMEWORK OF PALAUIG, ZAMBALES

This Chapter sets the framework and character of the Comprehensive Land Use Plan (CLUP) by stating the long term desire and aspirations of the people of Palauig. It presents the ideal state of the municipality through empowered decision-making and cooperation.

# 2.1 Vision Statement

The Vision Statement of Palauig indicates what its people hope and aspire the municipality to be in the next nine years and beyond. It serves as the guiding framework for all the major plans, program and policies. It was crafted on 22 September 2014 in a multi-sectoral visioning workshop. The final Vision Statement of Palauig reads as follows:

“We envision Palauig as the agri-industrial and tourism hub of Zambales;

- 1. achieved by sustainable livelihood and progressive economic programs,
- 2. with high regard to people’s quality of life and the promotion of social justice,
- 3. under a climate-resilient and ecologically-balanced environment,
- 4. supported by efficient and sound infrastructure development,
- 5. served by a public-spirited, transparent, and accountable local government.”

- 2.2 Vision Elements, Descriptors and Success Indicators

The vision statement illustrates the desired qualities of the municipality in terms of five (5) major elements, namely: 1) the quality of the people as individuals and as society; 2) the nature of the local economy; 3) the state of the natural environment with due consideration to DRR and CCA principles; 4) the condition of the built environment; and 5) the qualities of the local leadership.

# 2.3 Development Strategy

To achieve this vision the plan will depend on (2) vital development strategies:

- 1. An urban and rural development based on selective specific policy zones focusing on:

- • Preservative activities
- • Development activities that adheres to the principle of equitable distribution of growth in the municipality

- 2. A spatial development based on expansion-orientation that is agro-politan in nature (meaning, it aspires to the delocalization of primary production).

# 2.4. Spatial Development Framework

The policy objectives of the development framework preparation would also include the following:

- 1. Even up development concentration
- 2. Initiate development in underdeveloped areas as counter magnets
- 3. Arrest further urban decay and deterioration in the center
- 4. Promote environmentally-conscious urban development
- 5. Integrate appropriate infrastructure facilities, community amenities, and basic services into the projected development pattern

# CHAPTER 3 DEVELOPMENT CONSTRAINTS AND POTENTIALS

In this chapter, the present situation and conditions of the Municipality of Palauig are assessed through the municipality’s potentials for development that could be the advent for developmental plans and programs of the local government. Assembling on these potentials will help conceive different development goals and objectives. Furthermore, in spite of the numerous potentials of the town, there also exist several constraints which hinder development. The local government should therefore address these constraints so as to mitigate their impacts on the municipal development goals.

- 3.1 ENVIRONMENT SECTOR

- 3.1.1 CONSTRAINTS

- A. Natural Hazards (Flood Risk)

Flooding is a perennial problem in low lying areas in the municipalities of Palauig. The most common trigger event for the occurrence of flood in the municipality is the tropical cyclones that bring heavy rainfall. Flood prone areas in the municipality are along the adjacent areas of rivers and major tributaries due to inability of river channels to accommodate the large volume of run-off caused by excessive amount of rainfall. One of the primary contributing factors for the occurrence of these hazards is heavy siltation of waterways, resulting to the limited capacity to handle heavy flash flood water flow from the rain catchment areas.

Damage to properties and loss of profit from agricultural activities are some of the problems encountered by affected residents. When the banks of Rivers overflow, low lying barangays in Palauig such as San Juan, parts of Liozon, Locloc, Macarang, Bato, Alwa, Santo Tomas, Libaba, Garreta, West Poblacion, East Poblacion, San Vicente, Pangolingan and Magalawa are affected. A good environmental management plan is necessary to address natural hazards and mitigate their impacts.

- B. Improper Waste Disposal System

The collection of wastes is performed only for the urban barangays in the municipality while the rural barangays settle for open dumping sites. In addition, improper disposal of solid and liquid wastes as evidenced by the usual practice of throwing of wastes in

the river results in water pollution and water quality degradation. It was also noted that there are only a few Materials Recovery Facility (MRF) in Palauig as compared to the national standard, prescribed by law (RA 9003) of one MRF per barangay. Furthermore, the municipality is in need of a unified sewerage system in the urban area and its adjoining barangays.

# C. Unplanned Development

There are possibilities of unplanned development and conversion of the areas, which would bring about consequences to the water resources quality and land productivity. Since Palauig is an agri-based municipality, the preservation and proper management of lands and water should be highly considered. The municipality must protect its natural environment from unplanned and ecologically unsound development.

# D. Need to strengthen inventories and assessments on air and water quality, mineral resources, and biodiversity

Due to the lack of an environmental baseline database that should include inventory and assessment of the quality of air and water, minerals and flora and fauna, there has been a difficulty in fully comprehending the current environmental state of the municipality. However, Palauig is assessed to have a good water resource quality as assessed by the local water utility provider. This information will also facilitate proper environmental planning and management.

- 3.1.2 POTENTIALS

- A. Abundant Natural Resources

The large area of fertile land is devoted to agriculture. Palauig has many agricultural areas located in Brgy. Pangolingan, Bulawen, Liozon, Bato, Sto. Nino, Cauyan, San Vicente, Lipay, and some parts of Salaza, where forest areas are also seen. Aside from agriculture, fishponds are also predominant in the municipality particularly in Brgy. Libaba, Pangolingan, Sto. Tomas, Alwa and Macarang. Soil classifications such as swamps, marshes, and mountain soils is further classified into five groups namely: Bani clay, Bancal clay, Beach sand, Bulawen Clay loam, La Paz fine sandy loam, Mountain soils undifferentiated and Hydro soil. The Bulawen Sandy Clay Loam and

the Bancal Clay are the most predominant soil types while the Beach Sand and La Paz Fine Sandy Loam soil types can be found across the different barangays in the municipality. Palauig is also home to Mt. Tapulao in Brgy Salaza where important flora and fauna species can be found.

# B. Good Air Quality

Air pollution is not much of a problem in the municipality since there is no major air pollutive industry operating in the locality.

- 3.2 INSTITUTIONAL SECTOR

- 3.2.1 CONSTRAINTS

- A. Insufficient Baseline Data

Although a Community Based Management System is in place, there is a need to update its eco-profile in order to plan the development of its various sectors (i.e., social, economic, environmental, infrastructure and institutional).

- B. Inadequacy of capable technical managerial personnel

There is a need to augment the current workforce by hiring more personnel, such as nutritionist, environmental officer, information technology officer, etc.). The local government should enhance and develop the skills of its personnel through various management and technical trainings to be able to adapt to the constantly changing environment (locally and globally).

- C. IRA Dependency

The municipality of Palauig is a third class municipality that is heavily dependent in the share of the national government’s Internal Revenue Allotment (IRA). This condition can be improved if the local government will aggressively institute various fiscal management reform programs, such as: vigorous tax collection campaigns, fiscal incentives to induce local investments, cash flow management, etc.

- 3.2.2 POTENTIALS

- A. Government Administration

Majority of the plans and programs instituted by Honorable Mayor Generoso F. Amog are fully supported by the Planning and Development Office, including the different municipal division heads and barangay officials. The various NGOs and POs are also actively involved in the planning processes through their representatives who are members of the Local Development Council.

- B. Partnerships

The local government of Palauig has so far displayed a willingness to enter into partnerships with national government agencies (implementation of national government programs), foreign-funding institutions (infrastructure development) and the academe and experts (creation of local development plans). This shows that the local government has been able to assess their weaknesses and at the same time find solutions to address their deficiencies.

- C. Increase in Self-Reliance

The Local Finance Committee should come up with different options to increase the local income of the LGU aside from updating its tax map and increasing collection efficiency, which will lead to fund-generating enterprises to increase local income.

- 3.3 SOCIAL SECTOR

- 3.3.1 CONSTRAINTS

- A. Absence of Universities and Vocational Schools

There is no established tertiary education institution in the municipality. High school graduates from four secondary institutions in Palauig namely, Palauig Educational Foundation, Carmel Academy, Rofulo M. Landa High School, and Locloc National High School are likely to enroll in other public and private schools in the nearby cities and towns. Some would probably stop schooling and look for a job instead.

# B. Insufficiency of Classrooms and Teachers

In 2012, out of 4 secondary schools found in Palauig, only Carmel Academy (1:32) had complied with the 1:35 teacher-student ratio. The rest of the secondary schools were not able to hire and train teacher to comply with the standard.

Out of 4 highschools, only Palauig Educational Foundation was compliant with the standard of 1:35 classroom ratio. The rest of the high schools were not compliant and Rofula Landa High School posted 1:68 ratio indicating that classrooms in Palauig would not suffice given the number of high school enrollees.

There is much needed construction and rehabilitation of classrooms to support the increasing number of enrollees and school-going population.

# C. Health Issues and Concerns

Compared with the DOH standard ratio, the number of health providers practicing in the public health system is way below the standards. As of latest data provided by the Municipal Health Office (MHO), Palauig has 16 barangay health centers, with only one 1 male doctor with and 1 female nurse.

The leading causes of morbidity would be Acute Respiratory Infection, hypertension, and pulmonary tuberculosis while pneumonia is the leading cause of mortality.

Sanitary disposal of human waste can have a positive impact on reducing disease and mortality. According to the National Demographic and Health Survey (NDHS) report of the National Statistics Office (NSO), three (3) in five (5) households in the Philippines have a private flush toilet. Toilets that flush into a septic tank are much more common in urban areas than in rural areas, while pit latrines are more common in rural than urban areas. Some coastal barangays in Palauig municipality don’t have sanitary toilet facilities.

# D. Understaffed Protective Services Offices

There is a lack of patrolling personnel since there should be at least 1 police officer to serve 1000 people, as provided for by the standard for protective services. Palauig has 1 police officer for every 1,834 population.

The Palauig Fire Station employs 7 firefighters. The fire station utilizes 1 fire truck, which is unserviceable and is still undergoing repair. Similar to the situation of the police force in the municipality, the number of fire fighters is also inadequate. Moreover, there are no civilian volunteers at the station.

# E. Lack of Affordable Housing

Based on the latest household data, Palauig has 8,367 households and as per latest inventory of dwelling units, Palauig has only 6,507 houses. Out of 6, 507 only 6,157 are considered to be a livable dwelling unit. This means, housing backlog of Palauig is at 2,130 dwelling units.

- 3.3.2 POTENTIALS

A. Access to Health Services and Insurance

As of February 2014, there are sixteen (16) Rural Health Units (RHUs) in the municipality. The RHU II has an accredited outpatient department by PhilHealth. There is a declining trend in the prevalence of malnutrition, and improving health conditions among the population.

3.4 PHYSICAL & INFRASTRUCTURE SECTOR

3.4.1 CONSTRAINTS

- A. Congested Poblacion and Traffic

Generally, roads in the municipality are considered to be in poor condition. However, continuous monitoring and improvements are conducted by the Local & National government. Presently, there are about 6.1% of the existing roads that are gravel/earth-paved that need to be concreted or asphalted. Improvements of farm to market roads are also initiated.

The increasing number of motor vehicles creates poor traffic movement in the municipality. Sidewalks and some portions of the roads are being utilized as temporary terminals and parking spaces for both private cars and public jeepneys and tricycles. Some portions of the National Road are being widened in order to ease traffic.

Motor vehicles are the main means of transportation in the municipality. Tricyles (motorcycle with a sidecar) are the most common mode of transportation in Palauig because of low fare charges and numerous routes it can serve. It is also convenient for commuting short distances and feeder trips such as between residential area, municipal roads and adjacent barangays. There are over a thousand registered public motorcycles in Palauig. However, temporary tricycle terminals are situated alongside the commercial roads causing traffic congestion especially during peak hours.

# B. Poor Water Distribution System

Thirteen (13) out of the 19 barangays in the municipality are being served by the Palauig Water District. They are barangays: Liozon, Macarang, Bato, Alwa, Salaza, Lipay, San Juan, Garreta, Libaba, Sto. Tomas, East Poblacion and West Poblacion. Aside from this, the remaining barangays survive on traditional and improvised extraction methods, i.e., open well, shallow well, deep well, artesian well, manual and motored pump. Some depend on open well, pitcher type, jetmatic pump and spring as the major source of water supply in the community. These water supplies are extensively used to obtain water for bathing, washing and other domestic uses. It is a necessity that domestic and commercial consumptions must be in treated forms to prevent the outbreak of water-related diseases. Traditional methods of water treatment include heating, chlorination, and filtering.

# 3.4.2 POTENTIALS

- A. Well Maintained Roads The municipality of Palauig has approximately 122.7 kilometers of roads which is

classified into four categories. These are the national, provincial, municipal and barangay roads. The provincial roads constitute the majority length having 85 kilometers. The national roads follow with road length of 17.6 kilometers, the municipal roads with 14 kilometers, and the barangay roads with 6.1 kilometer. As to the type of pavement, the roads are classified as concrete, asphalt and gravel/earth filled. Concrete road type constitutes about 99 kilometers while the asphalt road type is 17.6 kilometer. The rest is made of gravel/earth filled which is about 6.1 kilometer.

# C. Well Established Bridges

While the provincial roads and bridges are all passable with the majority in good condition, continuous maintenance and upgrade is required regularly. Roads and bridges form the core of movement within and outside of Zambales and having passable roads assures the efficient movement of people, goods and services within the area. A bus terminal in Sta. Cruz and Iba caters to the transport services in and around the municipality of Palauig.

An inventory in 2014 noted fourteen (14) bridges within Palauig. The shortest is a 20meter long bridge while the longest measures 120 meters in length. These are located along the National/Local Road alignments, linking at critical sections.

# B. Modernization of Telecommunication

The available means of communication in the municipality are individual cell phones, 1 telegraph service station provided by government carrier; telephone services provided by national telecommunication providers catering domestic and international calls; and local/provincial postal service provided by the Philippine Postal Corporation. Their services include conveyance of correspondence, goods and monetary exchange services. There are also two radio stations and 9 cellular sites across the municipality by Smart Telecommunications, Globe Lines, and Sun Cellular Network.

Efficient services provided by the different telecommunication companies significantly contribute to the continuous growth of the municipality. The municipality is in a normally advanced stage of accurate coverage of local, national and international news and interests.

# C. Maximized Power Distribution

Palauig Electric Power is served by the Zambales Electric Cooperative (ZAMECO I). It is the only power utility company in the municipality. As of March 2014, all 19 barangays in the municipality have been served by ZAMECO I. The total customer count of the utility company is pegged at 51,695. These include residential, commercial, industrial, street lighting, public buildings and special lighting needs. Households that are not yet connected by the electric company use kerosene and other resources for their power needs.

- 3.5 ECONOMIC SECTOR

- 3.5.1 CONSTRAINTS

A. POVERTY INCIDENCE

Poverty incidence is the proportion of population whose annual per capita income falls below the per annual per capita poverty threshold to the total number of population (NSCB).

The annual per capita poverty threshold of the province of Zambales increased in a span of three years. Region III fared better than other regions in the Philippines by ranking third lowest (12%) in poverty incidence in a 2009 survey.

In 2003 and 2009, poverty incidence was highest in Palauig (24.4% and 21.3%), followed by Sta. Cruz (18.4% and 18.7%), Candelaria (17.4% and 18.2%) and Cabangan (16.7% and 21.2%). It was consistently lowest in Olongapo City (3.8% and 4.5%), San Narciso (8.88% and 10.7%) and San Antonio (10.84% and 12.0%). In 2009, only three other municipalities had lower than 13.0% (the provincial average). These are Subic (11.5%), Castillejos (12.5%) and the provincial capital Iba (12.5%). There are indications of a relatively low poverty incidence in San Felipe. Once again, the bipolar nature of Zambales becomes evident in that the more poor municipalities are in the north end and the less poor municipalities are in the south end. Poverty reduction programs can take their cue from these findings.

- A. Vast Agricultural Lands

Palauig is a palay producing town and considered as one of the rice bowl of the province. Palay being the main crop is being cultivated on 3.574 has of irrigated and non-irrigated land. Irrigated land has a total production of 8,360.74 MT per year while the none irrigated has a total production of 4,295.52 MT per year. Intercropping of vegetables and root crops are planted to vacant riceland on seasonal basis. Fruit trees like mango, cashew, and coconut have an area share of around 458.21 has. Double cropping of palay is regularly practiced by farmers who have sufficient water supply. Irrigation of this land comes from rivers, creek, and deep wells.

# B. Labor Force

Based on the 2012 data provided by the municipality, there are 25, 337 labor workers of Palauig. 67.27% of the population is employed while 32.73% of the population is unemployed.

# C. Excellent Fishing Potential

Fishing is the secondary occupation of the people in this municipality. Palauig which lies along the coastal line of the province has a tremendous fishing potential, China Sea, the river banks and inland waters are the fishing ground of the town. Brackish water has an approximate area of 39.00 hectares with total fish production of 27.3 Metric Tonnes, while the fresh water is around 35.17 hectares with total fish production of 670.34 Metric Tonnes

# D. Increased Trade, Industry & Services

The type of business or trade found in the municipality varies from retail trades, banks, and service such as recreational and personal services. At present, there are 238 business establishments registered in the municipality with the sari-sari stores having the biggest number.

The type of commercial area Palauig has is that of a Minor Central Business District where the shopping and service area is found in Barangay West Poblacion with the market as its main feature complemented by shops and offices as well as recreational and transportation facilities.

# E. Presence of Potential Tourism Areas

At present, Palauig has no fully developed tourist spot although there are many places to be tapped. These include the Island of Magalawa, shorelines in Brgy. Locloc, East Poblacion, San Juan, Garreta, Lipay and Sitio Luan and the proposed ecotourism site in Tapulao.

Mt. Tapulao is located at the North Eastern part of Palauig, Zambales with an altitude of 2, 037 meters above Sea level and a total land area of 5, 892 hectares, and was promoted to be the next summer capital of the Philippines. Mt. Tapulao as it is

# 4.2 Generation and Evaluation of Alternative Spatial Strategies

This section shows the considered alternative spatial strategies and the urban form suitable for the municipality of Palauig, in view of closing the gap between the desired status as envisioned in Chapter 2 and the development constraints pointed out in Chapter 3. In the development of municipal urban form, there are three (3) spatial strategies generated based on the existing development pattern of Palauig and presented to the Palauig Technical Working Group in June 2015. The spatial strategies take into consideration the results of the Climate and Disaster Risk Assessment following the Supplemental Guidelines

espoused by the Housing and Land Use Regulatory Board on the Mainstreaming Climate Change and Disaster Risk Reduction in the Comprehensive Land Use Planning Processes. These are the: 1) “Do Nothing Scenario”, 2) “Integrated Linear and Quaternary Development Focus” 3) the “Integrated Multi-Nodal Development Strategy Scenario”. The following chapter is a discussion and analysis of the general description of each spatial strategy and its positive or negative implications on the physical structure and environment of Palauig.

- 4.2.1 The “Do-Nothing” Scenario

![image 2](<784708893-PALAUIG-ZAMBALES-CLUP_images/imageFile2.png>)

a. General Description

Under this arrangement, urban growth with its current trends in the various sectoral activities will continue to expand freely in all directions emanating from the existing CBD without any method or approach for the development of the Municipality. This urban form clearly shows a development where the population is not restricted to any form of development within the area including the possibilities of building in restricted zones such as environmentally critical areas.

Due to the fact that the expansion and direction of the development will not be altered in any way, the other barangays away from the CBD will remain underdeveloped. Congestion in the center will definitely occur in the future due to migrants not only from the different barangays but also migrants from outside the municipality going to the one and only CBD in the area. As the population increases with local government may not be able to support the greater population in the area in terms of food, shelter, and other services.

As unplanned development continues with this scenario, chaotic networks of roads will ensue. Sprawl will without the consideration of the different hazards and environmentally critical and protected areas such as fault lines and floods that may lead to destruction or loss of life.

- 4.2.2 The Integrated Linear and Quaternary Development Focus

![image 3](<784708893-PALAUIG-ZAMBALES-CLUP_images/imageFile3.png>)

a. General Description

Accessibility will be a great advantage in this scenario since the proposed layout of the development will flow in a linear direction along existing National Roads. It will be

a hotspot for both foreign and local tourists thus increasing the municipality’s income generation outputs due to the four (quaternary) development focus: 1. CBD; 2. Business and Institutional Support Zone; 3. North Agri-industrial Support Zone towards the municipality of Masinloc and; 4. South Agri-Industrial Support Zone towards the municipality of Iba in Zambales. More business opportunities and locators fromboth local and regional investors can be expected.

However, urban sprawl will likely occur starting from the existing CBD across the municipality at a certain time period. In addition, this proposed development does not imply inclusive growth because the development shares the benefit of certain barangays only. Likewise, heavy traffic will likely occur in the future mainly along the national and provincial collector roads entering both sides of the development.

# 4.2.3 The Integrated Multi-Nodal Development Strategy Scenario

![image 4](<784708893-PALAUIG-ZAMBALES-CLUP_images/imageFile4.png>)

# a. General Description

The multi-nodal urban form is used to direct and manage the development away from the main urban core by introducing other potential satellite zones or development centers. Under this spatial form, the municipality of Palauig will develop different growth nodes which will also be classified by hierarchy.

With the application of this spatial strategy, equal distribution of services will most likely be met since the proposed development provides six other alternative development focus namely: 1) CBD; 2) Business and Institutional Support Zone; 3) North Agri-industrial Support Zone towards the municipality of Masinloc; 4) South AgriIndustrial Support Zone towards the municipality of Iba in Zambales; 5) Magalawa Tourism Development Area; and 6) Mt. Tapulao Tourism Development Area; all of which supporting processing and light-medium industrial development, business, institutions, and tourism activities which strategically sustain and serve all the barangays in the municipality. The strategy promotes a more organized distribution of goods and services.

From the defined location of the five growth nodes, the road patterns relative to their linkages will connect each development center to the existing urban core. In this situation, transportation will be more efficient, which will mitigate traffic congestion and provide a more adapted and adequate form of linkage in the delivery of goods from the satellite centers to the CBD.

This planning strategy supports the concept of decentralization which equalizes the distribution of the population as well as the benefits of development. # b. Mt. Tapulao Tourism Development Area

Mount Tapulao (also known as High Peak) is the tallest mountain in the Zambales Mountain range and in the province of Zambales in the Philippines. The trail to the top of Mount Tapulao is on rolling and gradual terrain that takes hikers through a number of distinct ecosystems: from lowland grass and scrubland dominated by talahib (cogon grass) to its flanks of secondary to primary dipterocarp forest, mossy montane forest and pine forest above 1,800 metres (5,900 ft) that extends to the adjacent mountains.

Mount Tapulao can be reached by trekking the mining road on a ridge on the side of the mountain with the forest line starting at about 1,875 meters. The jump-off point is from the Dampay resettlement area in Brgy. Salaza in Palauig, which is around 8 kilometers (5.0 mi) from the Zambales Regional Highway on rough road. From the trai lhead at Brgy. Dampay to the summit is around 18 kilometres (11 mi). Residents of Dampay are evacuees from the foot of Mount Pinatubo and are the "self-appointed guardians" of Mt. Tapulao under the coordination of the Municipal Tourism Authority of Palauig, Zambales. The ascent can be done in 7–8 hours while the descent can be covered in half the time. Guest and tourist aiming to climb the mountain are required to register at the barangay hall to receive further safety, security and cooperation according to the rule and guidance of the tourism authority. Farther down the settlement is a trail leading to the jump off-point for the Bagsit River whitewater river rafting.

# 5.1.6 Fishery Development Zone

The NAMRIA-generated Municipal waters’ boundary also serves as the fishing grounds of the Municipality and its coastline as fry collection area.

# a. Mariculture

Aquaculture is defined under RA 8550 as "fishery operation involving all forms of raising and culturing fish and other fishery species in fresh, brackish, and marine water areas". This plan defines the aquaculture zone as an area intended for fish and shellfish operations like fishpens, fish cages, and mussel and oyster culture beds. Following the provisions of RA 8550, the aquaculture areas should not exceed 10 percent of the municipal waters. The size allocation for the aquaculture structures should be agreed upon by the different stakeholders and users of the area, with the approval of the local government. The Municipality has big socio-economic potential for the establishment and management of this zone. However, impacts of aquaculture such as the excessive use of feeds that affect water quality have to be mitigated.

The zoning of areas that are presently devoted to mariculture has been adopted as a basic framework for the management and control of mariculture investments and structures. More importantly, zoning has been accepted as a direct strategy to prevent congestion of shallow coastal waters, coves and inlets to impose a state of ecological orderliness in the operation of fish and shellfish farms. In the end the ultimate goal is to preserve and sustain the ecological soundness of mariculture areas so that they can support highly productive mariculture indefinitely. Under this scheme, zones devoted exclusively for the establishment and operation of specific mariculture farms and similar ventures will be subject to explicit management guidelines, aquaculture ethics and clean production practices and a permitting system.

# b. Fishing Area

This zone refers to areas designated exclusively for fishing activities within municipal waters using small- and medium-sized fishing vessels of three (3) gross weight tons or less and employs traditional, non-active fishing gears such as hook and line (kawil), The designation of a traditional fishing zone considers actions to reduce multiple- use conflicts, maintain environmental quality and ensure the sustainable management and development municipal fisheries.

# 5.1.7 Habitat Enhancement and Protection Zone

Habitat Enhancement and Protection Zone in the Municipality of Palauig consists of mangrove, coral reef, seagrass and pawikan nesting ground. Mangrove areas are located in most of Palauig coastline stretching from Brgy. Lipay to Brgy. Liozon, including the island of Magalawa. Records from the DENR Region III on the total area of mangrove have yet to be finalized, with Brgys. Lipay and Magalawa the only site being ground validated.

# 5.1.8 Marine Protected Area Zone

The Municipality of Palauig proposed two sites as Marine Protected Areas (MPAs) within its Municipal Waters’ Boundary. They are located adjacent to the Palauig reef and near the mouth of Bagsit River, with a combined area of more or less 380 hectares.

The MPA zone is the core conservation zone where the habitat and its associated biodiversity are conserved, consistent with best practices in adaptive protected area management. The establishment of MPAs in municipal waters is a common coastal management strategy worldwide and is defined by the World Conservation Union as:

"any area of intertidal or subtidal terrain, together with the overlying water and associated flora, fauna, historical and cultural features, which has been reserved by law or other effective means to protect part or all of the enclosed environment."

Various local and international protocols support the establishment of protected areas in the marine environment. The Fisheries Code in particular, mandates that up to a maximum of 15 percent of municipal/city waters should be declared as either fish refuge or sanctuary, or fishery reserve. The Code has only briefly qualified the distinction between the two categories; a reserve being an area where activities are regulated and a refuge or sanctuary is an area where activities are prohibited. In practice, however, much of the fish sanctuaries and reserves established in the country are a combination of both areas; a core sanctuary where a no-entry, no-take policy is enforced; surrounded by a buffer zone where certain types of fisheries and human activities are permitted on a limited basis. Marine parks are also established in other areas particularly where various uses compatible with habitat conservation but includes tourism related activities. The following are the objectives in the establishment of this zone:

- • protection and maintenance of marine biological diversity and its associated natural resources;
- • enhancement of the potential for fish stock replenishment in the surrounding coastal waters through the allocation of enough habitats for protection of maturing species of fish;
- • protection of the remaining coral habitats and associated ecological structures;
- • provide a refuge for intensively fished species of fish;
- • protection of unique species under international protection; and
- • enhancement of the marine tourism potential of the surrounding areas.

In addition, conservation zones can be focal points for environmental research, re-seeding of appropriate marine organisms, and can be used as indicators to measure the progress of rehabilitation initiatives. The level of allowable activity in this zone can be expected to vary from one situation to another. The utilization of this area is in accordance with the regeneration of resources and functional characteristics of the area.

Palauig’s settlement areas are made up of subdivisions and self-built houses

Production areas are geared specifically for the provision of food for the municipality, cash crops for exportation, and development of tertiary industries. # 6.5 Easements of Public Use

Article 51 of the Philippine Water Code (PD 1067) stipulates:

The banks of rivers and streams and the shores of the seas and lakes throughout their entire length and within a zone of three (3) meters in urban areas, twenty (20) meters in agricultural areas, and forty (40) meters in forest areas, along their margins, are subject to the easement of public use in the interest of recreation, navigation,

floatage, fishing and salvage. No person shall be allowed to stay in this zone longer than what is necessary for recreation, navigation, floatage, or salvage or to build structures of any kind.

Considering that the municipality has a number of minor and major rivers, the abovementioned provision is adopted to prevent developments that may lead to the degradation and destruction of the waterways. Provisions in the zoning ordinance will be the main mechanism for its implementation.

The following policy and legislative intervention can be executed in order achieve the appropriate easements:

- i) Enforcement of Article 51 of the water code through the zoning ordinance;
- ii) Reclaim riverbanks that may have been built upon;
- iii) Construction and establishment of linear parks along the river that will serve as open spaces, measures for flood susceptibility, contributory effort in making the municipality ‘green’.
- iv) Prevent construction of structures along the riverbank.

# 6.5.1 National Road Easements

To prevent obstruction and traffic congestion caused by establishments being builtup too close to national roads, Presidential Decree 705 or the Forestry Code may be used to enforce a 20-meter regulatory setback from the edge of the ROW. Doing so would retain a significant allowance of open space preventing encroachments along the national road.
