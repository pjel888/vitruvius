# Part 1: WHEREAREWENOW?

### Profile of the Municipality

#### 1.1 HISTORY

In his book Conquistas de las Islas Filipinas, Father Gaspar de San Agustin records the Municipality of San Mateo in 1572 as a town annexed to Pasig. He described the inhabitants as "fierce but friendly and of quiet disposition." Father Juan de Medina, in his account Relacion de los Con ventos Y Pueblos Fundados por los PP. Agustinos, likewise put 1572 as the year the Parish of San Mateo was established. However, according to Miguel López de Legazpi, the first Spanish Governor in the Philippines (1571 to 1572), it was Juan de Salcedo, his nephew who discovered the site of present-day San Mateo during one of his expeditions to Manila from Cebu. Accounts say that two years before he came to Manila from Cebu, Legazpi sent Salcedo along with 150 soldiers to prepare the inauguration of the City of Manila. It must have been Salcedo who discovered the town before Legazpi inaugurated Manila on June 24, 1571.

Still, another account was that of Father Cavada, an Augustinian priest, who said that the first chapel in the country having the Patron Saint Matthew was built by the riverside in 1596 south of the present Poblacion of San Mateo which was then only a Barrio Tondo. Saint Matthew thus gave to the town its name when the chapel was set up during his feast day.

What may be gleaned from the above accounts somehow is that San Mateo was discovered sometime in 1571, while it was in 1596 when it acquired its first church. It may be true, too, that the Parish of San Mateo was set up in 1572.

Aside from the controversy over the date of its discovery or creation, there is also uncertainty as to how San Mateo acquired its name. According to one account, when the Spaniards made a reconnaissance of Manila's environs, they came upon the San Mateo flood plains and became enamored by the natural beauty of the place. They decided to establish a community in the place and one day, a Spanish scribe happened to stand on a mound with a book on one hand and a pen on the other. His statue-like pose prompted a companion to laugh and comment, "Muy" friend, the way you are now makes you look like Saint Matthew, referring of course to the usual way the evangelist saint is depicted in his statues. In the midst of their banter and merriment, they decided to call the place they discovered ‘San Mateo’.

Another account says that the Municipality of San Mateo was so called because of its geographical resemblance to another town in Spain with the same name. As described by Paluzie in a geographical book, this small Spanish town was near a high mountain (and) has a river that flows in its center which often floods but quickly recedes. This town is also a grazing ground for big animals and a fishing village. San Mateo is traversed by the Maly and Nangka rivers and its flood plains may have been a lush grazing area then.

The Augustinian priests in San Mateo were later formally replaced (in 1689) by the Jesuits who as early as 1637 included San Mateo as one of their missions, with the missions of San Isidro and Paynaan under it. The Jesuit Order brought with them an image of the Virgin Mary which came from the town of Aranzazu, Nueva Vizcaya, Spain. The Jesuits were the ones responsible for building the church now located in the Poblacion since the original chapel set up by the Augustinians beside the river was destroyed during a flood.

The location of the present church is in Barangay Sta. Ana and its patron saint is the Virgin Mary of Aranzazu. The original images of Saint Matthew which was housed in the old Augustinian chapel has been transferred to Barangay Dulongbayan (formerly llaya) and is now the patron saint of the Barangay and whose feast day is always celebrated on September 21.

Over the centuries, San Mateo has had a colorful and distinguished history. In 1639, a pitched battle ensued between Chinese rebels, on one hand and Spanish and native troops, on the other. The Chinese were defeated and retreated east to the Sierra Madre Mountains, but not before burning the town and its church.

On May 16, 1687, the territory and convent of San Mateo were added to Pasig by the Augustinians, with the headquarters and residences of the missionary at Mariquina (Marikina). Two years later, the Agustinians handed over the ecclesiastical administration of San Mateo to the Jesuits. In 1699, the convent of St. Augustine won in a court case against the native inhabitants with regard to a claim over a ranch in the district of San Mateo.

From 1696 to 1746, the town residents rebelled against the Spanish authority in the municipality. They were forced to go to the mountains and abandon the lowland settlement. However, in 1746, the townspeople returned to the lowlands after having been wooed and persuaded by the Jesuit priests.

The independent-mindedness of the San Mateo people showed itself again in the 15-year period from 1751 to 1765 when the natives rebelled against Spanish governance. The residents were ordered to surrender their weapons, but they refused. With this resistance, the Spanish government was forced to reduce the town to ruins.

In 1712, the Conde de Lizarriaga, the Governor of the Islands, sent Captain Don Lorenzo de Yturriaga together with 12 soldiers to punish Captain Pambila, a native chieftain who was reportedly inciting the residents to revolt against the Spanish government and the local Spanish priest. Captain Pambila attacked the Spanish officer, but the latter was able to parry the blow and shot the native leader dead.

San Mateo with its large size was dismembered on April 27, 1871 when Captain General Isquierdo issued a decree separating the barrios of Balite, Burgos, Marang and Calipahan from San Mateo and formed

- them into the new municipality of Montalban (Rodriguez).

During the Spanish Period, there was only one public school in San Mateo and the educational advancement of the youth was very slow. There were very few feathers and the books used were limited to the religious tracts called Eaton, Camia, Castiana and Doctrina Christiana After knowing how to read, most students quit school while a few who were wealthy enrolled in the friar schools in Manila.

In November 1896, during the revolution against Spain, General Llanera of the Filipino forces made San Mateo his headquarters. And on August 6, 1898, San Mateo joined the revolutionary government of Gen. Emilio Aguinaldo. During the Philippine–American War, Gen. Licerio Gerónimo's guerrilla bands from the foothills of San Mateo and Montalban attacked the American troops in October 1900. However, the Filipinos were defeated. A curious twist of history occurred when during the fighting, Gen. Lawton—the famous general who captured the elusive Indian rebel named Geronimo, among his exploits—was also killed by the forces of a Filipino bearing the same nameGen. Licerio Gerónimo.

In the eyes of the Filipino people, Gen. Geronimo became a great man for killing an illustrious American General who distinguished himself during the American Civil War and the American military campaign in Cuba as well as In northern, southern and central Luzon.

During the American Occupation. Act No. 137 of the Philippine Commission incorporated San Mateo into the newly created Province of Rizal on June 11, 1901. In line with its policy of fiscal economy and centralized governance, the Philippine Commission enacted Act No. 942 in 1903 which consolidated the municipalities of San Mateo and Montalban, with the former serving as seat of government. On February 29, 1908, Executive Order No. 20 separated San Mateo from Montalban, making the former an independent municipality. Its real divisions then included the barrios of Ampid, Sta Ana, Guitnangbayan, Dulongbayan, Malanday, Guinayang and Maly. However, due to constant development of the rural areas and the increase in population, the barrios of Guitnangbayan and Dulongbayan were divided into two sections.

With regard to education, it was in 1909 during the American Period when public schools were opened in the country under the supervision of Dr. David Burrows. San Mateo became one of the recipients of the benefits of the new democratized system of education when Mr. Frank Green was assigned to the town as the first school supervisor who was assisted by two Filipino teachers, namely, Jose Bernabe and Miguel Cristi. The English language was also taught in the public school. Transportation and other facilities in the municipality were increased and repaired thus resulting to more communication and exchange of goods and services.

During the Japanese Occupation, the Japanese Army occupied San Mateo, but no battle transpired between the Japanese and Filipino forces. There were only minor encounters between the Japanese soldiers and members of the Filipino guerrilla forces. Nevertheless, some prominent residents of the town were killed and maltreated by the Japanese military on suspicion that they were either members or supporters of guerillas.

Upon their arrival in the municipality, the Japanese forces recommended the appropriation of school buildings and some big houses and used them as their headquarters. Productive rice lands irrigated by water from the main pipe of the Metropolitan Water District have also been forcibly appropriated and tilled under the direct supervision of the Japanese officials. Livestock and crops were likewise confiscated to supply the food needs of the Japanese military in San Mateo and Montalban.

When the American forces landed in the north of Manila on February 3, 1945 and started recapturing the surrounding areas, San Mateo came under the line of fire of the U.S. Liberation Forces which was continued to be helped by Filipino soldiers under the Philippine Commonwealth Army and Philippine Constabulary and local guerrilla fighters. Residents were advised by the Air Raid Warden to vacate the Poblacion and seek refuge in Sitio Kalamyong on the west bank of the Marikina River. There they built temporary shelters while the U.S. Army Air Forces bombarded the Japanese Headquarters in Montalban everyday. However, one afternoon a lamentable thing happened when a bomb carried by one American Air Force plane was accidentally released over the evacuation area at Sitio Kalamyong. About one hundred civilians died from the accidental bomb explosion.

Republic Act No. 137 of the Philippine Commission incorporated this town into the newly created Rizal Province on 11 June 1901. In 1903, the Philippine Commission "in line with the policy of economy and centralization" as opposed to today's policy of decentralization enacted Act No. 942 which consolidated the Municipalities of San Mateo and Montalban, with the seat of government at San Mateo.

Executive Order No. 20, dated February 29, 1908 separated Montalban from San Mateo, thus formalizing the latter's status as an independent municipality.

###### 1.3.2 Topography

The topography of the municipality is characterized by a rolling to mountainous terrain on the eastern side and a flat terrain on the western side bordering Marikina River. The entire mountain areas of San Mateo has been declared “Risk Areas”, that is, from “low” to “high” risk, by five Philippine government agencies (PHIVOLCS, PAGASA, NAMRIA, NDCC, OCD) and by the UN.

###### 1.3.3 Land Forms

The municipality has two types of landform. The first is north-south trending strip of plain on the western side of the town which is traversed by Marikina, Nangka, Ampid and Maly rivers. The other type of landform is the rolling hills to the east that starts to rise from 1 to 2 kilometers from Daangbakal Street. 25 percent of the town area consists of lowlands while 75 percent of the area is the sloping and elevated terrain.

###### 1.3.5.1 Mineral Resources

Construction Material particularly concrete aggregates can be found in some barangays of San Mateo. This aggregate materials found in barangays Guinayang, Malanday and Guitnangbayan II, are composed of hydrothermally altered volcanics ranging from basalt to diabase. These are overlain by flat-lying to gently dipping beds of tuffaceous clastics. The contained spilitic diabase rock may be used for concrete aggregate, provided that fractured zones with pyrite can be segregated.

Two mining companies are currently operating in the municipality. Both started their operations in San Mateo in 2002. Conrock Development Corporation operates a mining site under a mineral production sharing agreement with the government in Sitio Patiis, Brgy. Malanday and Brgy. Guinayang. The leased

quarry area occupies 27.42 hectares where concrete aggregates are extracted and sold in Metro Manila.

Just beside the Conrock Development Corporation mine site in Sitio Patiis, Malanday is one quarry area of Monte Rock Corporation covering an area 18.075 hectares and also extracting concrete aggregates stones.

A smaller area of 4.99 hectares in Sitio Abuab, Brgy. Guitnangbayan II is also being mined for the same materials, which are supplied to the nearby Metro Manila construction/ real estate markets.

###### 1.3.6 Climate

The climate in San Mateo belongs to the Type I climate pattern, characterized by a relatively dry season from December to May and wet during the rest of the year. The northeast monsoon brings cool, relatively dry winds. The municipality is not directly hit by typhoons, strong winds and low pressure systems because of the protection by the Sierra Madre Mountain ranges on the east and by the Batangas and Laguna mountains on the southwest. Cooler temperatures can be experienced at the eastern highland section of the municipality.

###### 1.3.7 Hydrology

###### 1.3.7.1 Surface Water

Rivers and creeks are present in San Mateo. This surface water follows the contour of the area moving from east to west leading to Marikina Valley. It moves from east to west forming a tree-like branching out pattern or a dendritic pattern. Marikina River which starts from Rodriguez in the north and ends south in Laguna de Bay, is the biggest and most important river in San Mateo. It has a total drainage area of 538 square kilometers. It serves as a means of

transportation and as irrigation to nearby farms in the alluvial plain. Marikina River tends to overflow during protracted monsoon rains and typhoons.

Maly River acts as the northern tributary of Marikina River. It flows from east to west and drains the Pintong Bukawe watershed. Maly River fills the ponds in the low-lying areas of the flood plain before flowing to Marikina River. This river forms part of the boundary of San Mateo with Rodriguez. South of the Maly River and flowing east-to-west through Barangays Guinayang, Malanday, Dulongbayan II, Guitnangbayan I and II is Abuab Creek.

The northern upland portions of the town is drained by the river, including the Mt. Mataba area. Ampid Creek is the other natural water channel that drain the southern hilly areas of Barangays Silangan, Sto. Niño, Gulod Malaya and Ampid. This creek flows through the populated portions of the town. Nangka River acts as the boundary of San Mateo and Marikina City, flowing from east to west. It has a drainage area of 76 square kilometers. This river flows through many built-up areas in San Mateo and Marikina sides of the river. This reduces the threat to the residents by lessening the likelihood of getting polluted and silted.

###### 1.3.7.2 Ground Water

According to National Water Resources Council (NWRC), the part of municipality located in Marikina Valley belongs to the shallow well areas. This area is suitable for constructing wells with depths of not more than 20 meters and are recommended for rural water supply, particularly Levels I and II services. According to their study, shallow well areas are those with a geologic structure consisting of recent formations and having a predominant slopes of 0 to 3 percent. Shallow wells are mostly situated at elevation within 50 meters above sea level as in alluvial and coastal plains and

river valleys. They are less prone to saltwater intrusion and bacteriological pollution compared to deep wells of the same discharge and location but shallow wells may not be resistant to the effects of fertilizers and agro-chemicals, especially those that are located near rice and vegetable fields.

However, although they may be made safe from bacteriological pollution, they may not be resistant to the effects of fertilizers and agro-chemicals, particularly those that are constructed near rice and vegetable fields.

The static water level (SWL) in shallow well areas is generally within 6 meters below ground surface (mbgs). In the case of San Mateo, the SWL average is 8.60 mbgs with a range of 5.48 to 10.67 mbgs. In term of well depth, the average depth being dug is 50 meters, with a range of 18 to 139 meters. With regard to specific capacity, the town has an average of 1.02 liters per seconds (lps) per meter, with a range of 0.41 to 2.75 lps per meter.

###### SITE TRIP GENERATION

Site trip generation is defined as one-way movement from a point of origin to a point of destination. It can be classified into the following categories: (1) Purpose; (2) By Time of day and (3) By Person Type.

In the case of the proposed AUV terminals, the purpose of trip will be based on the regular activities of the residents near the proposed terminals. Second in consideration will be the time of the day. Since it will be a transport hub, it is expected that influx of passengers/ commuters will be visible in a 24/7 basis. Lastly, person type covered of this study were mainly composed by office workers and students.

Trip purposes may vary depends on the clientele per day. Possible reasons for trip were as follows:

- • Employment- it is composed of the working group starting from the age of twenty-one (21) years old up to sixty-five (65) years old. The usual destinations for these people seeking employment are areas in Makati, Ortigas, Quezon City and Bonifacio Global City in Taguig or commonly known as Central Business Districts;
- • Education- this is composed of students from elementary up to college level outside San Mateo. The proposed terminals shall prioritize means of transportation to the students bound to Katipunan Area where three universities/schools are situated. In addition to this, the terminals shall also cater to students who will be travelling to Taft Avenue in Manila where the known “University Belt” is located.
- • Religion Purposes- this specific trip is composed of people who travel outside to visit churches and other religious sites for their spiritual needs. Common places that were being visited by people and devotees in San Mateo are churches of Our Lady of Peace and Good Voyage Shrine in Antipolo City, Minor Basilica of the Black Nazarene- Parish of Saint John the Baptist Church also known as the Quiapo Church in Manila and National Shrine of Our Mother of Perpetual Help or commonly known as Baclaran Church. Influx of demand usually occurs during Lenten Season.
- • Leisure- this category includes acquiring other basic needs or alternative of goods and services to nearby shopping centers and mall establishments such as but not limited to SM City Masinag, Sta. Lucia East Grand Mall, Robinsons Metro East, Ayala Feliz Mall, SM City Marikina, Gateway Mall, SM Megamall,

- Robinsons Galleria, Glorietta/ Greenbelt Mall, and malls located within the Bonifacio Global City.
- • Special Trips- these are trips that were not included in the categories mentioned above. It includes hospitalization services, cemetery, and the like.

On the other hand, trips being generated by the students and employees from the nearby commercial, industrial and institutional areas may cause indirect traffic effect in the area since all of the proposed transportation terminals have close proximity to residential areas.

Having identified all the possible trips within the area as well as its surrounding vicinity, location of all the terminals will have the possibility to be full of activity from 8:00 in the morning until 5:00 in the afternoon and can be extended even in the late hours of the night.

###### 1.4.5.2 Waste Diversion

According to their 10 Year Solid Waste Management Plan, the Municipality is aiming 75% waste diversion wherein most of waste will be recycled instead of going straight to the landfill.

Residual plastics wastes are diverted to CemEx Philippines through its TSEK (Tamang Segregasyon para sa Kalikasan) Program. TSEK is a waste segregation initiative that also promoted the collection and recycling of dry plastics.

Dry biodegradable wastes are diverted to the Municipality’s Charcoal Briquetting Livelihood Project, while the compostables go to the Municipal Agriculture Office (MAO) vermiculture composting. The vermiculture composting project of the MAO will be a support system for a long-term backyard vermicomposting for container gardening of all households and barangays.

For recyclable wastes, the Municipality has a partnership with TzuChi Foundation for the collection of recyclables. San Mateo also has an eco-bag livelihood program that promotes eco-friendliness and waste diversion, producing bags and other products made out of tarpaulins, foils, tetra packs, etc.

The increase of waste diversion target per year will be favorable for the municipality especially to the lifespan longevity of Sanitary Landfill located in Barangay Pintong Bukawe.

###### 1.4.5.3 Sanitary Landfill

The San Mateo sanitary landfill owned and operated by the San Mateo Sanitary Landfill Development Corp. (SMSLDC) is located in Barangay Pintong Bukawe. It has a total area of eight hectares for the two phases. Phase I has an area of three hectares while Phase II has an area of five hectares which can accommodate about 5,000 tons of residual waste daily. The landfill also has leachate holding ponds, a water-treatment facility that produces clean water to grow fish and plants, and a material recovery facility that recycles garbage.

#### 1.6 TOURISM SECTOR

Tourism is one of the strengths of San Mateo, and this is one of the major priorities of the current administration. Because of the mountainous terrain of San Mateo, it can be a venue for many recreational activities. Cultural tourism may also be heightened since there are identified tourist spots in the locality such as the Diocesan Shrine and Parish of Nuestra Señora de Aranzazu.

###### 1.6.1 Declared Tourism Sites

According to the primary data of the Municipal Tourism Office, tourism here is both composed of leisure and cultural components. As of today, the Ciudad Christhia Resort is certified by the Department of Tourism (DOT), and the Diocesan Shrine and Parish of Nuestra Señora de Aranzazu is declared as a national treasure by the National Historical Commission of the Philippines. There is an on-going move to declare the Nuestra Señora de Aranzazu Church as a Department of Tourism accredited tourist spot.

#### 1.9 DEVELOPMENT CONSTRAINTS: PRIORITY ISSUES AND CONCERNS

###### A. Need To:

- • Improve Traffic Circulation and Linkages

There is a need to decongest the main thoroughfare and provide more parking spaces for commercial establishments along the highway. Traffic and lack of parking give negative perception to prospective business locators to the area. There is also a need to provide more roads to link the remote parts of San Mateo in order to have better functional integration and inter-connectivity within the Municipality.

- • Provide Affordable Housing Affordable housing must be made accessible especially to Informal Settler Families (ISF), families without housing units and families living in hazard-prone areas.

- • Manage Development and Set the Limits to Growth

With San Mateo’s rapid growth and urbanization, there is a need for programs and projects to be planned for sustainability and a need to limit areas for development so that future growth can be accommodated without ruining the environment and depleting San Mateo’s natural resources.

###### B. Major threats to future development

- • Increase in population and population density will result in additional demand for urban land and for infrastructure and utility services.
- • Presence of natural hazards (e.g., East and West Valley Faults, flooding, landslide) and significant built-up expansion in disaster-prone areas (e.g., high-risk mountain areas).

#### 1.10 FUNCTIONAL ROLE OF THE MUNICIPALITY

San Mateo belongs to the Small/Medium City (Primary Urban Center A) category and have strong linkages to Metro Manila and Quezon City.

San Mateo is seen to evolve as a Secondary Metropolitan Center whose role is that of an international production and distribution center, joining Antipolo City and the municipalities of Binangonan and Rodriguez as locations of medium and large industries, commercial centers, tertiary hospitals and government centers and schools.

The municipality is also identified as a primary ecotourism destination because of its environment and natural setting which are ideal for recreational and extreme sports activities such as biking, hiking, paragliding, All-Terrain Vehicle (ATV) off-road riding, and swimming.

#### 2.3 THE CONCEPT / STRUCTURE PLAN

###### 2.3.1 Spatial Strategy

San Mateo will adopt a combination of centripetal and off-center pivot form, and nodal form for its spatial development.

The centripetal and off-center pivot form reflects an outward expansion of urban development from the city center induced by the construction of the new C6 circumferential road. With development originating from the center, this spatial pattern redirects future development to extend to the adjoining barangays. As a result, the direction of growth enlarges the urban core. For San Mateo, the presence of its steeper slopes at the eastern side of the Poblacion will serve as a natural control to urban expansion, limiting urban sprawl and preserving protected forest areas.

![image 210](<844057380-Copy-of-San-Mateo-Rizal-clup_images/imageFile210.png>)

In the nodal form, the major center provides specialized facilities and services to its nodes and acts as its external linkage to the other centers of the municipality. The nodes support the major center as its captive market while providing neighborhood facilities and services to its area of influence. Under this spatial form, a number of additional mixed-use growth areas will be developed outside the Poblacion area or existing center of development. These new growth areas are envisioned to attract settlers from the Poblacion in order to decongest the town center, as well as accommodate the influx of in-migrants from neighboring cities and municipalities.

The three (3) identified nodes are the Timberland Development by Filinvest Land,

Inc., the proposed new Educational, Cultural, and Convention Center, and the Forest Area.

#### 2.5 COMPREHENSIVE DRAINAGE PLAN

The municipality of San Mateo was heavily affected by Typhoon Karding last August 2018 and had been placed under a state of calamity due to the destruction brought about by the heavy monsoon rains. About 80% of the town was submerged by floodwaters. Residents were evacuated and were housed in temporary shelters. The water level at the river reached 23.14 meters way above its critical level of 20 meters.

In response to this latest calamity, the Municipal Engineering Office of San Mateo formulated a Comprehensive Drainage Plan to provide possible solutions to the flood problems of the municipality.

###### 2.5.1 ANALYSIS

![image 250](<844057380-Copy-of-San-Mateo-Rizal-clup_images/imageFile250.png>)

Storm Runoff Map

The Municipal Engineering Office conducted an analysis of the cause of flooding brought by Typhoon Karding. The analysis brought about a number of possible explanations for the flooding within the municipality.

Due to rapid development in San Mateo, the holding capacity of the upland and lowland areas continues to reduce, and largely contributes to the water runoff. There is a huge contribution of water runoff from upland and lowland areas that goes down to the municipality’s river basin. Some of the boulders eroded from the upland areas often block the canals during heavy rainfalls. With the continuous increase in rainfall intensity and the combined runoff contribution of upland and lowland areas, the river is not able to meet the required capacity to accommodate the run-off.

![image 252](<844057380-Copy-of-San-Mateo-Rizal-clup_images/imageFile252.png>)

![image 253](<844057380-Copy-of-San-Mateo-Rizal-clup_images/imageFile253.png>)

The runoff volume of the upland areas is about 324,480 cu. m. or equivalent to 130 Olympic size swimming pools. The current volume storage capacity of Sapang Labo is 80,000 cu. m. From the computed runoff volume from the upland areas, the spillage will be 244,480 cu. m. or equivalent to 98 Olympic size pools. The required depth to accommodate this amount of run-off will be 20.28 meters, a difference of 17.78 cu. m. from the existing 2.5 meters.

###### 2.5.2 RECOMMENDED SOLUTIONS

San Mateo, through its Sangguniang Bayan, has recommended the following solutions, together with the corresponding approved Resolutions:

- A. Moratorium: A proposed moratorium on all upland development, quarry operation and any low land development until such time as San Mateo’s overall drainage system has been totally established. This can ensure that the increasing water discharge caused by the rapid town development can be contained in the proposed drainage system.

- B. Interceptor Drainage Box Culverts

#### 2.6 TOURISM AND HERITAGE CONSERVATION

###### 2.6.1 Cultural and Heritage Tourism

The town of San Mateo has a rich cultural history, which has given it a unique Spirit of Place. There are many historical buildings, religious processions, festivals, and activities that contribute to its rich heritage. On August 28, 2018, the Diocesan Shrine and Parish of Nuestra Señora de Aranzazu was declared as a national treasure by the National Historical Commission of the Philippines and the Historical Marker was unveiled on August 31. For these reasons, many local and international tourists alike flock to visit San Mateo to experience its Spirit of Place and take part in its festivities, giving a big boost to the Tourism industry of San Mateo.

In order to preserve this unique character and rich cultural heritage, the local government of San Mateo has issued Municipal Ordinance No. 052-S-2016 declaring the area within a 100-meter radius of the Nuestra Señora De Aranzazu Church as “Special Protective Zone”.

This CLUP update proposes to initially expand the coverage area of the Heritage Zone to around 7 hectares. There is a need for larger assembly space for tourists, participants, and observers of festival events in and around the Church. Pedestrianization of some areas adjacent to the Church and creation of plazas will help address this need.

The planned expansion of the Heritage Area will be via an amendment of the existing ordinance which will expand the 100-meter radius to 1000 meters.
