# Occupancy Classification

## Definition

Occupancy classification is the categorization of buildings or portions of buildings based on their intended use, function, and associated level of risk to life, safety, and property.

## Why It Matters

Occupancy classification is one of the most fundamental determinants in building design and regulation. It directly affects:

- allowable building height and area
- fire-resistive requirements
- egress and life safety provisions
- light and ventilation requirements
- sanitation and occupancy load limits

Incorrect classification can lead to non-compliance, unsafe conditions, and rejection of building permits.

## Canonical Explanation

Under the National Building Code of the Philippines and its Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR), buildings are grouped into occupancy classifications based on use.

These classifications define the baseline requirements that must be met for a building to be considered compliant.

Typical occupancy groups include:

- residential occupancies
- commercial occupancies
- institutional occupancies
- industrial occupancies
- assembly occupancies

Each classification carries its own set of requirements for:

- fire safety
- structural considerations
- means of egress
- allowable density and use

The classification is applied to the entire building or to specific portions, depending on the design and usage.

## Rules / Conditions / Exceptions

- Mixed-use buildings must classify each portion separately and comply with the most restrictive applicable requirements where interactions occur.
- Change of occupancy requires re-evaluation of compliance with current code provisions.
- Certain occupancies require stricter fire-resistive standards due to higher life safety risks.
- Temporary or special occupancies may have additional or modified requirements.

## Notes

Detailed classifications and definitions are specified in Rule VII of the IRR.

Concept-level understanding should always be cross-checked against the IRR for exact definitions and requirements.

Different editions or interpretations may refine classification boundaries, particularly for mixed-use developments.
